# CVAH Veterinary Assistant University — Charter

Version 1.0 · 2026-08-17

## Mission
Systematically convert new hires into safe, technically competent, clinically useful veterinary assistants in approximately 6–8 weeks, and continue developing them through specialty credentials — so that the veterinary team can confidently transfer real clinical workload to them without compromising patient safety.

## The Central Outcome
A newly hired assistant with little or no prior clinical experience becomes a safe, useful, technically competent general-practice veterinary assistant in ~6–8 weeks when ability and clinical exposure permit. Exceptional learners may progress faster; learners needing more exposure may extend to an outer developmental window of ~16 weeks with structured, stigma-free remediation. **Sixteen weeks is never the default. Calendar time never graduates anyone.**

## The Fundamental Design Question
Every module, skill, assessment, and dashboard must answer:
> What appropriate clinical workload can this employee safely and reliably take off another team member's plate after mastering this competency?

The University optimizes for: safe independence within permitted scope; clinical usefulness; technical reliability; good judgment; situational awareness; patient advocacy; proper escalation; workflow efficiency; and trust from the veterinary team. It explicitly does **not** optimize for completion rates.

## The Three Questions of Competence
No competency is granted unless the learner has demonstrated all three:
1. **Can you explain it?** — anatomy, physiology, equipment, purpose, risk, workflow, safety principles.
2. **Can you do it?** — correct, consistent, safe, efficient physical performance, observed and scored against a defined rubric.
3. **Can you recognize when it is not going correctly?** — failure, artifact, patient distress, equipment malfunction, contamination, complications, inappropriate conditions, unexpected findings, and the point at which to stop and escalate.

A learner with perfect quiz scores who cannot perform has not graduated. A naturally talented learner who cannot recognize complications has not graduated.

## Conflict-resolution hierarchy
When expert roles disagree, resolve in this order:
1. Patient safety
2. Legal scope (Arizona law and Board rules)
3. Educational effectiveness
4. Practical hospital usefulness
5. Maintainability
6. Affordability

## Schools of the University
1. **Core Veterinary Assistant Academy** (mandatory, ~6–8 weeks)
2. Advanced Perioperative & Anesthesia Academy
3. Laboratory & IDEXX Academy
4. Veterinary Dentistry Support Academy
5. Diagnostic Imaging Academy
6. Emergency & Critical Care Support Academy
7. Inpatient & Hospitalized Patient Care Academy
8. Pharmacy & Medication Handling Academy

Schools 2–8 are optional stackable post-core credentials. There is deliberately **no Trainer or Leadership Academy** in this version; operational tooling for assessors/managers exists without a leadership curriculum.

## Credential disclaimer (must appear on every certificate and transcript)
> CVAH University credentials are internal hospital competency designations. They are not state licensure, state certification, or professional credentials, and they do not change the holder's legal scope of practice under Arizona law.
