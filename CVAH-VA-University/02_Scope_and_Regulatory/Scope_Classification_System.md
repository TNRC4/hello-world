# Scope Classification System

Version 1.0 · 2026-08-17

Every task in the University carries exactly one scope class and one supervision requirement. These appear in the skill spec, the lesson, the rubric, and the passport. Learners are taught their own classifications explicitly (Week 0).

## Scope classes
| Code | Class | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| K | Educational knowledge only | Learner understands it; never performs it |
| O | May observe | May be present and watch |
| A | May assist | May support a DVM/CVT performing the task |
| P | May perform under specified supervision | May perform once passport state permits, under the stated supervision level |
| HP | Hospital-policy dependent | Legal under supervision, but CVAH policy decides; requires explicit policy entry |
| CT | Credentialed-technician restricted | Reserved to CVT (or DVM) by law, rule, or CVAH policy |
| DVM | Veterinarian restricted | Diagnosis, prognosis, prescription, surgery, and anything CVAH reserves to DVMs |
| JD | Jurisdiction dependent | Classification differs by state; Arizona classification given, flagged for any multi-state reuse |

## Supervision: what the law says, and what CVAH adds

**Arizona recognises exactly two supervision states.** They are defined in A.R.S. § 32-2201 and there is no third:

| Statutory term | A.R.S. § 32-2201 | Definition (verbatim sense) |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct supervision** | (8) | A licensed veterinarian is **physically present at the location** where animal health care is being performed. |
| **Indirect supervision** | (11) | A licensed veterinarian is **not physically present** at the location but **has given written or oral instructions** for treatment of the animal patient. |

Two further definitions govern who is who: **supervising veterinarian** (21) is the licensed veterinarian responsible for care rendered by a CVT or veterinary assistant; **veterinary assistant** (26) is an individual who provides care under the direct or indirect supervision of a veterinarian or certified veterinary technician.

**CVAH's operating levels sit inside those two.** They are hospital policy, not law. They exist because "in the building" and "at your elbow" are not the same teaching situation — but they may only ever be *stricter* than the statute, never looser.

| Code | CVAH operating level | Maps to statute | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Veterinarian or CVT physically present and directly overseeing this task | **Direct** (§ 32-2201(8)) | Training states 4–6; all first performances |
| S2 | Supervisor physically present at the location, immediately reachable, not watching this task | **Direct** (§ 32-2201(8)) | Default for most granted technical competencies |
| S3 | Supervisor not physically present; written or oral instructions given and communication available | **Indirect** (§ 32-2201(11)) | Only where the scope-matrix row explicitly allows and CVAH policy permits |

> Do not read S1/S2/S3 as a legal ladder. Legally there are two rungs. S1 and S2 are both *direct supervision*; only S3 is *indirect*. Any document, dashboard, or conversation that implies Arizona has three statutory tiers is wrong and should be corrected.

Under **A.R.S. § 32-2211(5)** ("Exceptions from application of chapter"), a veterinary assistant employed by a licensed veterinarian may perform duties **other than diagnosis, prognosis, prescription, or surgery** under the direct or indirect supervision of the licensed veterinarian who is responsible for the assistant's performance. The University's language never uses "independent" in a legal sense; the highest passport state is **"Independently Assignable Within Hospital Policy,"** which always sits inside a defined supervision level.

**What § 32-2211(5) does not do.** It is a general exemption, not a task list. It does not authorise any specific procedure. Tasks carrying meaningful patient risk — anything on the RISK-TIER-2 list in the scope matrix — must be settled by current Board rule and explicit CVAH policy, never by extrapolation from this sentence.

## Non-negotiable exclusions (always DVM)
Diagnosis · prognosis · prescribing/selecting/altering treatment plans · surgery · any procedure Arizona law or Board rule reserves to veterinarians. Nothing in this University implies otherwise, and completing the University changes no one's legal scope.
