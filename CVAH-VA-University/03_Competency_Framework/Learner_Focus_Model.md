# Learner Focus Model — Now / Next / Later

Version 1.0 · 2026-08 · Governs every learner-facing surface: the console, the LMS, printed packs, and what a trainer puts in front of someone.

## The problem this solves

The repository is exhaustive on purpose — 75+ skills, 30 lessons, 10 skill courses, 8 rubric documents, seven specialty academies, and a governance layer. That depth is an asset for the hospital and a liability for a person on their fourth day, who opens it, sees the scale of what they don't know, and quietly concludes they will never get there.

**The repository may stay exhaustive. The learner's view must not be.**

## The rule

> A learner sees **1–3 active competencies at a time.** Everything else is one deliberate click away, and nothing else is on their screen by default.

Three, not thirty, because that is roughly what a person can genuinely hold while also doing a job. A fourth active competency does not accelerate anyone; it just moves where the failure happens.

## The three bands

**NOW — what you are working on today (1–3 items).**
For each: the skill course, what a good attempt looks like, how many supervised reps you've logged, and what the next state transition needs. Nothing else. This is the entire default view.

**NEXT — what opens when Now is done (3–5 items, visible but not active).**
Named so progress feels real and the learner can see the shape of the path — deliberately *not* clickable into full detail, so it can't become a fourth active thing. "Coming up" is motivation; "also do this" is overload.

**LATER — everything else (collapsed).**
The full library, all specialties, every rubric, the governance layer. One click. Available to anyone curious, pushed at nobody. Trainers and managers work here routinely; learners visit it when they choose to.

## What this does not change

**The nine graduation gates are untouched.** This is a presentation model, not a standards model. Every gate in `Graduation_Requirements.md` stands exactly as written, and no gate may be softened, merged, or made optional in service of a simpler view. Simplifying what a learner *looks at* is not the same as simplifying what they must *prove* — conflating the two would be the failure mode of this whole idea.

**VA1/VA2/VA3 remain competency milestones, not a calendar.** Nothing here introduces week-based progression. The bands are populated by passport state and prerequisite readiness, not by date. A fast learner's Now band refills early; a learner needing more reps keeps the same Now band without penalty and without a visible countdown.

## How the bands are populated

Rules, applied in order:
1. Anything in passport state 11 (Remediation Required) or 13 (Temporarily Restricted) goes to **Now**, always, ahead of new material. Nothing new opens while something is broken.
2. Fill remaining Now slots with skills whose prerequisites are met, ordered by the workload the hospital most needs transferred (from the Clinical Workload Ownership Matrix) — so the learner's effort and the hospital's need point the same way.
3. **Next** = the skills those Now items unlock.
4. Everything else = **Later**.
5. A safety module never counts against the 1–3 limit; those are gates, not competencies, and blocking them behind a queue would be perverse.

## Trainer override

A trainer may pin an item into Now ("you're in dentals all week — let's use it") or park one out ("your grandmother is in hospital; the catheter work can wait"). Overrides are logged with a one-line reason, because a pattern of parking the same skill is itself a finding for the pilot loop.

## Success test

A learner should be able to answer, without scrolling and without asking: **"What am I working on, and what does 'good' look like?"**

If they cannot, this model is not implemented correctly — regardless of how complete the repository behind it is.
