# W0-02 — Hospital Safety: OSHA Basics, Sharps, Zoonoses, Chemicals

**Why this matters:** Your safety gates everything else — an injured assistant helps no patients. This module's assessment requires 100%.

**Objectives:** locate and use safety equipment; state sharps rules; state bite/scratch protocol; identify zoonotic risks; read an SDS; state anesthetic-gas and radiation basics (awareness level).

**Core instruction:** **Sharps:** never recap by hand; sharps container within arm's reach BEFORE any needle is uncapped; container swapped at fill line, never pressed down. **Bite/scratch protocol:** stop, wash immediately (soap, running water, minutes not seconds), report to supervisor SAME shift, document per OSHA log, medical evaluation per policy — cat bites especially are medical events, not badges of toughness. **Zoonoses awareness:** ringworm, leptospirosis, toxoplasmosis (pregnant-staff counseling point), scabies, roundworm/hookworm, rabies (vaccination status policy `[CVAH-SPECIFIC INPUT REQUIRED]`); the defense is boring: gloves, hand hygiene, PPE per task, no eating in treatment. **Chemicals:** SDS binder/portal location; formalin handling (fume awareness, spill kit); disinfectant dilution discipline. **Anesthetic gases:** scavenging exists to protect YOU; report smell of agent; pregnant-staff policy pointer. **Radiation:** awareness now, full module Week 5 — never in the room during exposure until trained and badged. **Ergonomics/animal handling injuries:** lift with help ≥ house weight limit `[CVAH-SPECIFIC INPUT REQUIRED]`; floor work posture; never catch a falling patient with your back.

**What doesn't look right?** Overfilled sharps container; recapped needle in trash; colleague rinsing a bite for two seconds and moving on; propped-open isolation door. **Escalation:** every injury reported same shift, no exceptions; near-misses reported too (they're free lessons).

**Clinical case:** A coworker gets a minor cat scratch, says "it's nothing," keeps working. Two days later: swollen hand, urgent care, out for a week. What should have happened in the first five minutes? (*Wash thoroughly, report, document, evaluate — cellulitis from cat scratches escalates fast.*)

**Knowledge check (100% required):** 1) When may you recap a needle by hand? (*Never.*) 2) First two actions after any bite? (*Immediate thorough wash; report same shift.*) 3) Where is the SDS collection? (*[location — tour answer].*) 4) When may you be in the X-ray room during exposure? (*Only after radiation-safety training and badging, and only when your presence is required with full PPE.*)

**Competency connection:** prerequisite to everything; feeds TS-SAN-001/002. **References:** OSHA bloodborne/sharps standards; NIOSH veterinary safety guidance; AAHA safety resources. **Version:** 1.0 2026-08-17.
