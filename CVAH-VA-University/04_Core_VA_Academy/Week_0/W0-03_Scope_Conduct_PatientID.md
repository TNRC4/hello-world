# W0-03 — Your Scope, Professional Conduct & Patient Identification

**Why this matters:** Knowing what you may NOT do is as load-bearing as any skill. And the single most catastrophic error class in hospitals is treating the wrong patient.

**Objectives:** state the four things Arizona law reserves absolutely; explain supervision levels S1–S3; use two-identifier patient checks every time; state confidentiality basics.

**Core instruction:** **Arizona scope:** as a veterinary assistant you may perform delegated duties **other than diagnosis, prognosis, prescription, or surgery**, under the direct or indirect supervision of a veterinarian who is responsible for your performance (A.R.S. § 32-2211(5)). Practical translation: you never tell a client what a condition is, what will happen, or what drug fixes it; you never alter a treatment; nothing you do here is surgery. CVAH policy further restricts some legal-ceiling tasks — your passport + scope matrix are the truth, and "may perform" always names a supervision level.

**Two words that mean something specific in Arizona law** (A.R.S. § 32-2201): **direct supervision** (8) means the veterinarian is *physically present at the location* where the care is happening. **Indirect supervision** (11) means they are *not* physically present but have given written or oral instructions. That is the whole legal vocabulary — two states, not a sliding scale. CVAH adds finer operating levels *inside* those two (S1 and S2 are both legally direct; S3 is indirect), because being in the building is not the same as standing at your elbow — but the law recognises only the two. When in doubt: ask. Asking is a competency, not a weakness — it's literally on your rubrics. **Patient ID:** two identifiers, every interaction: patient name + client last name (or hospital ID band/cage card ID). Verbalize at procedure start ("This is Bella Chen for a nail trim?"). Litter-mates, same-breed boarders, and "the other black lab" are how wrong-patient events happen. Cage card + patient + record must agree before anything touches the patient; any mismatch = full stop until resolved. **Conduct:** confidentiality (cases discussed only for care, never socially/socially-media); clients hear observations only in approved language; professional appearance/phone policy per handbook.

**Knowledge check (100% on items 1–2):** 1) The four absolute exclusions? (*Diagnosis, prognosis, prescription, surgery.*) 2) Cage card says "Max Jones," record open on screen says "Max Johnson." You were told to give Max his pill. Do you? (*No — full stop, resolve the mismatch first.*) 3) A client asks "is it cancer?" You suspect the mass looked bad. Response? (*"That's a question for Dr. X — I'll make sure they talk with you."*)
