# W1-02 — Canine & Feline Handling: Reading Before Touching

**Why this matters:** Handling is the platform under every other skill you will ever learn here — and the main way staff get hurt and patients get sensitized.

**Objectives:** read canine/feline stress signals and name the escalation ladder; perform safe approach; apply low-stress fundamentals; know muzzle/towel indications; state when restraint is becoming unsafe.

**Core instruction:** Delivered with **CSC-RES-01/CSC-RES-02** (the teaching courses) plus skill spec **TS-HND-001**; demonstration schedule for TS-RES-001/002 this week. Signal ladders: *canine* — lip lick/yawn/look-away → whale eye/freeze/tuck → growl/snap → bite (each rung skipped by us pushes the patient down the ladder faster next visit); *feline* — ears rotate/flatten, pupils blow, tail lash, crouch-freeze → hiss/swat → explode. The handling triangle: environment (quiet, non-slip, escape-audited), technique (least restraint that achieves stillness), and timing (procedures batched — one good hold, three tasks). Muzzles: indication is risk, application is calm and brief, basket vs sleeve, never on vomiting/dyspneic patients. Towels: burrito variants demonstrated live. Carrier disassembly beats extraction.

**Visual teaching:** INT-07 body-language image set (20 photos, drag label→signal); VID-HND-001, VID-RES-001/002.

**What doesn't look right?** "He's fine" while the dog is frozen with whale eye (he is not fine); a cat pupil-blown and silent (the explosion is pre-paid); three people leaning on one small dog (plan failed — stop, rethink: sedation is a kindness conversation for the DVM, not a defeat).

**Escalation:** red-line signals → CVT before contact; any bite/scratch → W0-02 protocol; any patient needing >2 handlers → DVM sedation conversation.

**Clinical case:** Fractious-cat room: carrier dumped, cat explodes, escapes into cabinets, 40 minutes lost, everyone scratched, cat now unexaminable — replay it correctly. (*Towel ready, top-off carrier disassembly, exam largely in carrier bottom, minimal fixation, batched procedures.*)

**Knowledge check:** 1) First three canine appeasement signals to name on sight? (*Lip lick, yawn, look-away — any of the early set.*) 2) When is scruffing a cat the CVAH default? (*It isn't — towel techniques first.*) 3) Restraint is becoming unsafe when…? (*Escalating panic, open-mouth breathing/cyanosis, anyone calls stop, plan requires force to continue.*)

**Practical assignment:** 5 coached approaches (mixed temperaments), 2 towel wraps, observe 1 muzzle application. **Competency connection:** TS-HND-001 → state 5; TS-RES-001/002 → state 4. **References:** low-stress handling literature (Yin); Fear Free modules (licensed separately if hospital subscribes); AAHA behavior guidelines. **Version:** 1.0 2026-08-17.
