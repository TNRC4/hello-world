# W2-05 — Inpatient Basics & Treatment Workflow

**Why this matters:** Hospitalized patients are watched by whoever is in the ward — which is often you. Week 2 makes you a safe pair of ward eyes and hands for the routine tier.

**Objectives:** read a treatment sheet fully; execute basic scheduled care (feeding per orders, walking, bedding, TPR checks) with initials discipline; give PO meds per TS-MED-PO-001 as granted; recognize the "different than an hour ago" reflex; hand off properly.

**Core instruction:** Read **TS-INP-001** (spec) + **TS-MED-PO-001**. This lesson adds the routine-tier boundaries: which patients are yours at VA1–2 (stable, non-ICU, non-isolation until trained) and which never are yet; the feeding matrix (NPO flags, post-op timing per orders, "ate?" quantified); walking rules (surgical patients per orders ONLY, double-leash flight-risk policy, never off-slip in corridors); the comfort layer (dry bedding, clean patient, water per orders) as medicine, not housekeeping — recumbent-patient turning schedule awareness (deep skill in Inpatient Academy).

**Clinical case:** 14:00 sheet says walk + feed post-op GI dog. Kennel shows vomit you didn't see happen. Feeding anyway because "the sheet says so"? (*No — new abnormality → report first; the sheet was written before the vomit existed. Sheets are orders, not oracles.*)

**Knowledge check:** 1) Sheet-cell rule? (*Every cell done-and-initialed or escalated — never blank/later.*) 2) You find NPO on the sheet after the patient begged adorably. (*NPO is NPO. Log the beg for morale.*) 3) When does "looks different to me" justify a report? (*Always — fresh-eyes escalation is the design, not an imposition.*)

**Practical assignment:** 2 supervised ward blocks; 5 PO administrations coached; 1 structured handoff given. **Competency connection:** TS-INP-001 → 5; TS-MED-PO-001 → 6–7; VA1 grant checkpoint end of Week 2. **Version:** 1.0 2026-08-17.
