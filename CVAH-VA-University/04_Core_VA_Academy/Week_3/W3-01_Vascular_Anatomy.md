# W3-01 — Vascular Anatomy & the Physiology of a Good Sample

**Why this matters:** People who understand the vessel hit the vessel. People who understand the blood keep the sample usable. This is the knowledge floor under Weeks 3–4's high-practice blocks.

**Objectives:** locate cephalic, lateral/medial saphenous, and jugular vessels on diagram and live patient; explain occlusion physiology; explain hemolysis/clotting causes at each step; choose site by patient/test/volume.

**Core instruction:** **The map:** cephalic — dorsomedial antebrachium (your everyday vessel: convenient position, modest volume); accessory cephalic Y; lateral saphenous — oblique across distal lateral tibia (dog's backup, rolls like a cable); medial saphenous — feline superstar (big, flat, fragile); jugular — the volume/quality vessel (groove low in neck; carotid deep-medial — why superficial technique + low occlusion matter). INT-04 interactive: label all vessels on 6 images (dog/cat × 3 sites), then hotspot "where do you occlude?" **The physiology:** occlusion → venous return blocked → distension (takes seconds — impatience reads as "no vein"); >90 s occlusion → hemoconcentration (skews PCV/TS/K+ — release and re-occlude on long hunts); negative pressure physics — vein walls collapse at the suction YOU choose; RBC shear (hemolysis) from small-needle+hard-pull, stopper-punch transfers, shaking; the clotting clock starts at needle entry — anticoagulant tubes are a race measured in seconds-to-a-minute.

**Site selection logic (taught as a decision table):** volume needed (jugular for >3–6 mL or multiple tubes), patient factors (dyspnea → no jugular positioning; catheter-planned limb → never cephalic there; coagulopathy → no jugular at all), test factors (coags want clean single-stick), temperament (cat medial saphenous under towel beats a wrestling cephalic).

**Knowledge check:** 1) Why can a perfect stick still hemolyze? (*Downstream handling: hard aspiration, stopper-punch transfer, shaking.*) 2) K+ oddly high on a long-occlusion draw — connection? (*Hemoconcentration/hemolysis artifact from prolonged occlusion.*) 3) Dyspneic cat, needs CBC/chem: site shortlist? (*Medial saphenous in gentle sternal/lateral-modified with CVT guidance — never jugular extension.*)
