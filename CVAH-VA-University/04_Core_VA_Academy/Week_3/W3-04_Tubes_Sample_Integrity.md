# W3-04 — Tubes, Fill Order & Sample Integrity

**Why this matters:** A perfect stick into the wrong tube, shaken, is a redraw — meaning another needle in the patient because of the last 20 seconds of workflow.

**Objectives:** match tube to test class; execute fill order; mix correctly; label at patient-side; name the preanalytic error from its artifact; prep samples for the reference lab.

**Core instruction:** The content is **TS-SAM-001** — this lesson wraps it in drills. INT-05 interactive: drag test-orders onto tubes (CBC→lavender, chem→red/SST per house, coag→blue-to-the-line, glucose-special→gray) with instant explanations; the fill-order sequencing game; the artifact gallery (photos: hemolyzed vs lipemic vs icteric serum; clotted EDTA feathering) with "name the cause" items. Reference-lab block: requisition matching, spin-and-separate timing, cold-pack vs ambient per directory, courier cutoffs `[CVAH-SPECIFIC INPUT REQUIRED: house courier schedule]`.

**Clinical case:** CBC flagged "platelet clumps — count unreliable." Draw was smooth. What probably happened and what will you change? (*Slow transfer or weak mixing — EDTA tubes get blood FIRST and 8–10 gentle inversions IMMEDIATELY; in cats, faster is everything.*)

**Knowledge check:** 1) Why is a short-filled blue tube garbage? (*Citrate:blood ratio fixed at 1:9 — underfill = relative citrate excess = falsely prolonged times.*) 2) Serum vs plasma — which tube gives which? (*Red/SST clot → serum; heparin/EDTA anticoagulated → plasma.*) 3) The three commonest hemolysis causes in-house? (*Aspirating too hard, stopper-punch transfer, shaking.*)
