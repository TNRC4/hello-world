# W4-01 — IV Catheterization Foundations

**Why this matters:** Venous access is the front door to fluids, anesthesia, and emergency drugs. Where CVAH policy permits assistants to place catheters, this is the Core's most advanced transfer; where it doesn't, this knowledge still makes you the best catheter assistant and monitor in the building.

**Objectives:** name catheter components and gauge logic; explain why the flash lies (stylet vs catheter tip); explain site strategy (distal-first); state the shear rule; assist a placement end-to-end.

**Core instruction:** Read **CSC-IVC-01** §§1–5 (the teaching course; TS-IVC-CEPH-001 governs). Component tray-teach: catheter/stylet unit anatomy, T-port, cap, flush; gauge color logic and flow physics (radius⁴ — why one gauge matters so much); the geometry lesson: catheter tip trails the stylet tip, so flash ≠ catheter-in-lumen — the advance-then-thread beat is the whole skill. The shear rule taught as absolute: **never re-advance a pulled-back catheter over its stylet** (fragment embolism); withdraw as a unit, always. Site strategy: start distal, fail upward. Policy checkpoint: your floor role this week depends on scope row SCOPE-004 — placement track or assist-and-maintain track; BOTH tracks complete this module.

**Knowledge check:** 1) Flash appears — why not thread instantly? (*Catheter tip may still be outside the lumen; advance unit 1–2 mm flat first.*) 2) Catheter won't thread; you've pulled it halfway back. Legal next moves? (*Remove catheter AND stylet together as a unit; never re-advance over the stylet.*) 3) Why distal-first? (*A blown distal site leaves the proximal vein usable; the reverse doesn't.*)

**Practical assignment:** model-limb lab: 5 placements to "smooth thread" standard. **Competency connection:** TS-IVC-CEPH-001 → 3–4. **Version:** 1.0 2026-08-17.
