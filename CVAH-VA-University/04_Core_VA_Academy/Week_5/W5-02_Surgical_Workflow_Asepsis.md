# W5-02 — Surgical Workflow & the Logic of Asepsis

**Why this matters:** Surgery days are choreography. An assistant who knows the steps — and treats sterility as physics rather than superstition — makes the whole OR faster and safer.

**Objectives:** narrate the surgery-day patient journey; explain contamination mechanics; apply sterile-conscience rules; identify who does what during a routine procedure.

**Core instruction:** The day's arc: admit/consent check → pre-op exam & bloodwork review (DVM) → premed (CVT/DVM) → induction → prep (YOU, TS-SUR-PREP-001) → transport → OR roles (surgeon sterile; anesthetist at the head; circulator — often YOU — the unsterile hands of the room) → recovery staging (YOU prep warm, quiet, monitored bay BEFORE the patient needs it) → turnover. **Contamination mechanics:** bacteria travel by touch, droplet, particulate rain (hover = rain), and moisture wicking (strike-through); the 2.5 cm wrapper border rule; below-waist/below-table = gone; doubt = contaminated (declaring it is a PASS behavior, concealing is the fail — this is drilled culture, W5-04 tests it). **Sterile field awareness for circulators:** traffic patterns (never between two sterile fields/backs), door discipline (each opening = particulate turbulence), speaking-distance behavior over open trays.

**Knowledge check:** 1) You *think* the glove tip brushed the drape edge. Nobody saw. (*Declare it. Zero-penalty culture exists precisely for this moment.*) 2) Circulator's cardinal movement rule? (*Face sterile fields, pass unsterile-to-unsterile side, never cross over.*) 3) Why does recovery get set up before induction? (*Recovery is the highest-risk anesthesia phase; scrambling for warming and monitoring after extubation is too late.*)

**Practical assignment:** shadow one full surgery day with role-map worksheet. **Competency connection:** unlocks TS-SUR-PREP/STER floor work. **Version:** 1.0 2026-08-17.
