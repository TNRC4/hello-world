# W6-04 — Emergency Foundations: Recognition, Activation, Your Role

**Why this matters:** Emergencies are rare per-day and certain per-career. The difference between a good and bad outcome is often the first 60 seconds — recognition and activation, which is exactly the assistant layer.

**Objectives:** recognize the emergency presentation set; execute the activation sequence; perform your CPR support roles (TS-EMR-001); stage equipment fast; know when normal workflow STOPS.

**Core instruction:** **The recognition set (drilled with images/video INT-10):** respiratory distress (effort, posture — orthopnea/elbow-abduction, open-mouth cat = emergency until proven otherwise, cyanosis); shock pattern (pale/grey gums, CRT >2s, weak fast pulses, cold extremities, dull mentation); hemorrhage (external obvious; internal pattern = shock + distending abdomen); seizure (active vs post-ictal, protect-don't-restrain doctrine, time it); collapse; heat emergency (panting hard + T high + collapse context); GDV silhouette (big-dog unproductive retch + distending drum belly = minutes matter); urinary obstruction (male cat straining/crying/no urine); toxin arrivals (bring-the-package doctrine). **Activation:** loud, specific, located: "DR. NOW — TREATMENT — HIT-BY-CAR" beats "um, can someone…"; one activation beats three whispers; false alarms are cheap, delays are not. **Your roles:** clear the table, oxygen rigged (flow-by immediately — mask/cage per direction), crash cart pulled, IV setup staged, sample tubes out, recorder/runner/compressor per TS-EMR-001 and the CPR lesson block (RECOVER-aligned; numbers live on CRC-002). **Workflow-stop rule:** an activated emergency outranks every routine task you own; your routine patients get flagged to a teammate, not abandoned silently.

**Knowledge check:** 1) Open-mouth breathing cat in lobby. (*Emergency activation + minimal-stress O2 — do not "get a weight first."*) 2) Big dog retching nothing, belly tight. (*GDV pattern — activate NOW; this is a minutes-count surgical emergency.*) 3) During CPR you're compressor; you're tiring at 90 seconds. (*Say it early, swap at the 2-min cycle — quality beats ego, per RECOVER.*)

**Practical assignment:** timed equipment-staging drill (O2 + cart + IV setup <3 min); mock activation; recognition image set ≥90%. **Competency connection:** TS-EMR-001 → 6–7; gateway to ECC Academy. **References:** RECOVER 2024. **Version:** 1.0 2026-08-17.
