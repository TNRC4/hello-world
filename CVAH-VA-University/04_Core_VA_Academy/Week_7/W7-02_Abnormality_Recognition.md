# W7-02 — Abnormality Recognition Intensive

**Why this matters:** Question 3 — *can you recognize when it is not going correctly?* — is the graduation question. This block is its final training push.

**Objectives:** score ≥85% on the recognition battery (with all critical items correct); demonstrate live "notice-and-report" behavior; calibrate your urgency judgments.

**Structure:** Day A — the battery primer: 40 practice items across domains (vitals trends, catheter sites, sample artifacts, restraint escalation, recovery patterns, pump/fluid faults, sterile-field violations, emergency presentations), each with explanation and urgency-tier reasoning (report-now / report-soon / note-and-monitor). Day B — media round: photo/video/audio set (INT-10 + INT-09 extended) under time pressure. Day C — the live gauntlet: trainers stage 6 findings across the training areas during your normal duties (a "wet wrap," a mis-labeled tube on the bench, an "expired" pack in rotation, a pump set clamped oddly, a treatment-sheet cell pre-initialed for a future time, a "patient" [sim] with new tachypnea); you're scored on noticing, urgency-tiering, and SBAR-V quality. Missed items are walked back with the trainer the same day — this is training, and then Day D re-tests near-misses. The formal battery (13_Assessments) follows.

**Calibration doctrine:** over-reporting beats under-reporting at your stage — but the goal is calibration: each battery item's explanation states WHY its tier is what it is, and your personal miss-pattern report (from the LMS) tells you whether you skew toward missing or over-flagging quiet findings.

**Competency connection:** graduation gate 5. **Version:** 1.0 2026-08-17.
