# CSC-RES-02 — Feline Restraint
Companion to TS-RES-002/003, TS-HND-002/004 · Benchmark course · ~25 min + floor practice

## 1. Purpose
Cats are not small dogs; they are a different physics problem. A cat gives you a short window of tolerance, spends it fast, and remembers how you used it. The CVAH standard is minimal-restraint, towel-first handling: the least contact that achieves stillness, with an exit ramp planned before you start. Teams that master this stop "losing" cats — into cabinets, into fear-aggression, into unexaminable-forever.

## 2. Prerequisite knowledge
Feline escalation ladder: ears rotating/flattening, pupil dilation, tail-tip flick → lash, weight shrinking backward, the crouch-freeze → hiss/swat → explosion. The cardinal rule: the silent, dilated, frozen cat is CLOSER to the explosion than the hissing one — hissing is negotiation; silence is a countdown. Physiology: stressed cats decompensate fast (they're often sick underneath); open-mouth breathing in any restrained cat is an emergency stop, full stop. Anatomy: twenty claws move in six directions; the towel exists because hands cannot cover that geometry.

## 3. Equipment
Two towels minimum (one working, one spare); non-slip mat; cat muzzle (doubles as eye cover — many cats settle blind) per policy; treats/lick paste; the CARRIER ITSELF (its bottom half is an exam surface a scared cat trusts).

## 4. Patient preparation
Read through the carrier door before opening anything: forward-and-curious vs pressed-to-the-back vs silent-and-huge-pupiled. Room prep BEFORE the carrier opens: door closed, window/screen audit, counters clear of avalanche objects, quiet voices, towel unfolded within reach. The carrier protocol: never dump, never drag. Unclip/unscrew the top half; lift it off slowly with the towel draped over your forearm ready to lay across the cat. Half of feline medicine can happen with the cat sitting in its own carrier bottom under a towel.

## 5–6. The holds
### The minimal hold (default for exams, most treatments)
DO: cat sternal ("meatloaf") on mat or in carrier bottom; your two forearms bracket its body like a valley, hands resting lightly over shoulders; no gripping unless movement starts. WHY: cats push against fixation and relax under mere boundaries. EXPECT: the cat compresses into a loaf and stays. COMMON ERROR: pre-emptive scruffing of a calm cat — you just spent tolerance you didn't need to. STOP: escalation ladder climbing past tail-lash.
### The towel burrito (draws, injections, anything a paw could reach)
DO: towel flat on table; cat placed sternal on its center-back edge; wrap side A snugly over the back and under the far side of the body; wrap side B over and tuck under the near side — snug through the shoulders, looser at the ribs (breathing room); leave out ONLY the part being worked on (a hindlimb for medial saphenous; the head for oral meds). Head covered or uncovered by the cat's vote — try covered first; most settle. WHY: containment without compression; a hide-option built in. EXPECT: a cat-shaped log that breathes easily and struggles briefly then settles. COMMON ERROR: wrapping a panicking cat TIGHTER as it fights — compression on a stressed cat is a respiratory event brewing; snug ≠ compressed. RECOVERY: struggle inside the wrap → open it, let the cat re-loaf, re-wrap once calmer or change plan. STOP: open-mouth breathing = wrap open NOW, all restraint off, CVT alert.
### Scruff — the exception, not the default
House policy: towel techniques first; scruff only as a brief last-resort control during an active safety event, never as the opening move, never suspended, never on seniors (skin tears) or obese cats (it doesn't even work). If a plan REQUIRES a scruff to complete, the plan is wrong — regroup (chemical restraint is the DVM's kinder answer).
### Holder role for feline draws
Medial saphenous and jugular holds per CSC-VEN-02/03; your feline deltas: contact points minimal, your murmuring voice constant, your eyes on the face (YOU are the respiratory monitor), and the stop-call is yours to make early.

## 7. Tactile medicine
Under your forearms, a settling cat softens and widens (the loaf spreads); a loading cat narrows and coils — you'll feel the spine arch beginning and the rear feet gathering under. Tail-lash transmits through the table. The wrapped cat's breathing is your hands' business: you should feel the ribs cycling easily through the towel; effort you can feel = wrap too tight or patient in trouble — open it.

## 8. It didn't work — now what?
**Cat won't leave the carrier:** top-off protocol; work in the bottom half; if it must come out, towel-over-and-scoop as one bundle. **Explosion mid-procedure:** let go of the PROCEDURE, not the room — drop the towel over the cat where it lands, dim, everyone silent for 60 s; then reassess: is the remaining task essential today (DVM call)? **The cabinet cat:** do not reach into the hole after it (that's how hands get shredded); close the room, open the cabinet fully, towel ready, patience or a DVM sedation plan. **Repeated visits, worsening behavior:** flag the record with SPECIFIC triggers observed ("panics at clipper sound; fine with towel-covered head") — you are writing the next visit's plan. **The genuinely dangerous cat:** stop protecting your pride — gloves-and-net folklore era is over; the answer is chemical restraint prescribed by the DVM, requested EARLY, framed as welfare (it is). **Open-mouth breathing at ANY point:** everything stops; O2 nearby; CVT/DVM now; the procedure no longer exists.

## 9. Real patients
**Kittens:** all containment, no force; socialization stakes are sky-high — pair every handling with food. **Seniors/CKD (your most common inpatients):** fragile skin, sore joints, often dehydrated — pad everything, no scruff ever, warm the towel. **Hyperthyroid:** wired, fragile hearts — shortest possible events, calmest room. **Brachycephalic cats (Persians):** airway margin is thin; sternal only, face never buried in towel, watch nostrils. **Diabetic/sick cats in for serial draws:** they learn fast — invest in making draw one pleasant (medial saphenous under towel + food) and draws two through ten stay easy. **The "fine at home" cat:** believe the owner AND the ladder in front of you; carrier-trained calm evaporates in clinic — read the current cat, not the story.

## 10. Common rookie mistakes
1. **Scruff-first defaults** — trained out with towel drills. 2. **Dumping the carrier** — the relationship-ender; top-off, always. 3. **Tightening a panicking wrap.** 4. **Working slowly** on a calm cat — feline tolerance is a countdown; be READY (supplies staged) then be brief. 5. **Reading silence as consent** — the frozen cat's explosion "from nowhere." 6. **Chasing an escaped cat** around the room — herd and towel instead. 7. **Skipping the room audit** — discovering the open window mid-explosion.

## 11. Pearls & banned folklore
PEARLS: warm towel from the dryer = instant 20% calmer cat. · Feliway/pheromone spray on towels per stock — cheap margin. · The carrier-bottom exam saves more scratches than any glove. · Do the worst thing LAST (temp after draw, not before). · Chin-scratch test: a cat that accepts one is telling you your window is open; one that jerks away is telling you it's closing. BANNED: scruff-suspension, full-body compression "hugging," spray bottles, gloves-as-substitute-for-plan (bulky gloves crush cats and miss holds), "cat whisperer" bare-hand bravado on flagged cats.
