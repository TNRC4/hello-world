# Clinical Skill Course (CSC) Standard

A CSC is the full teaching layer for a major technical skill — the instruction an excellent technician gives while standing beside a new employee. The TS-* specification remains the structured competency object (governance: scope, stop criteria, critical fails, repetitions, sign-off); the CSC carries the *how*. Lessons and specs point into CSCs; quick-reference cards are separate products and never substitute for these.

## Mandatory sections
1. **Purpose** — what, why, where it sits in workflow, what bad performance costs.
2. **Prerequisite knowledge** — just enough anatomy/physiology/equipment/safety to make the physical procedure understandable.
3. **Equipment** — everything, with sizes, choices, and why one item beats another.
4. **Patient preparation** — assessment, positioning, restraint, site prep, difficulty flags.
5. **Operator preparation** — where you stand, where the holder stands, which hand does what, equipment orientation, line of sight, anticipated patient movement.
6. **The procedure** — every meaningful step as a DO / WHY / EXPECT / COMMON ERROR / RECOVERY / STOP block. EXPECT includes what it looks, feels, and sounds like.
7. **Tactile medicine** — the physical sensations of the skill, taught so hands-on coaching lands on prepared ground.
8. **It didn't work — now what?** — the troubleshooting core: what you see/feel, procedural vs patient vs equipment causes, what to check first, what you may correct, when to change approach, when to hand off, when to escalate.
9. **Real patients** — how technique changes across the variations that matter for THIS skill.
10. **Common rookie mistakes** — what, why beginners do it, consequence, prevention, recognition, correctability.
11. **Experienced-staff pearls** — safe, earned procedural insights; unsafe folklore explicitly named and banned.
12. **Visual assets** — what exists or the production spec (storyboard/shot list), including correct / incorrect / borderline examples.
13. **Assessment & competency links** — knowledge check pointers, practical rubric, case-log targets.

## Quality gate — the three internal reviews (recorded at the foot of each CSC)
R1 Experienced technician: what practical detail is missing? what would I still have to teach? R2 True novice: do I know where to stand, what to hold, what success and failure look like? R3 Hospital utility: what can CVAH now trust this person to do that it couldn't before? A CSC ships only when all three have answers written down.
