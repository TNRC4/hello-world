# TS-HND-001 — Safe Approach & Low-Stress Handling

**School/Level:** Core/VA1 · **Prereq:** none · **Scope/Supervision:** P/S2 · **Revalidation:** none (foundational; refreshed via annual safety module)

**Equipment:** treats, towels, non-slip mats, slip leads, carrier tools. **Patient considerations:** species-typical fear signals differ (canine: lip lick, whale eye, freeze, tucked tail, growl; feline: ears flat/back, pupil dilation, tail lash, piloerection, hiss, freeze-then-explode). Pain changes everything — assume a painful patient may bite regardless of history.

**Anatomy/physiology:** flight distance and sensory zones; fear physiology (catecholamine surge → escalating reactivity; each bad event sensitizes the next visit).

**Indications:** every patient interaction. **Do not proceed:** patient showing red-line signals (hard stare + freeze, continuous growl, feline open-mouth threat) without CVT/DVM handling plan; dyspneic patients (minimal handling doctrine — get help first).

**Procedure:** 1) Read the room before touching — body language scan from door. 2) Approach obliquely, low, avoid direct eye lock; let dogs approach you where feasible. 3) Talk low and steady; move at 80% normal speed. 4) Test-touch on shoulder/side, not face/paws. 5) Keep between-patient handling breaks; reward calm. 6) Cats: towel available before opening carrier; disassemble carrier top rather than dumping/dragging. 7) Announce all transitions to teammates ("lifting now"). 8) Doors/escape audit before any leash removal.

**Common mistakes:** looming over dogs; scruff-first defaults on cats (not CVAH standard — towel techniques first); silent grabs; ignoring early signals until explosion. **Failure modes/troubleshooting:** escalating fear → pause, reduce stimuli, retry once with change (location, treats, second handler); still escalating → escalate to CVT (sedation decisions = DVM).

**Stop criteria:** red-line signals; any bite/scratch (stop, report, wound care per OSHA policy); patient panting/open-mouth (cat) or dyspnea. **Escalation:** any red-line patient; any injury; any patient needing more than 2 handlers.

**Cleanup/documentation:** note temperament observations in record using behavior-descriptive language ("hissed on towel approach"), never labels ("evil"). Behavior notes save the next handler.
