# TS-IVC-CEPH-001 — Cephalic IV Catheter Placement (Canine & Feline)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| School / Level | Core Academy / VA2 (state ≥8 typically post-core consolidation) |
| Prerequisites | TS-VEN-CEPH-001 ≥8; W4-01/02/03 passed; CVAH policy row SCOPE-004 permits assistants `[CVAH-SPECIFIC INPUT REQUIRED]` |
| Scope class / Supervision | P/HP · S1 through state 8; S2 at state 9–10 |
| Revalidation | 24 months, or 6 months without logged placements |

## 1. Equipment
Over-the-needle catheters 24 g (cats/small), 22 g (most cats/small dogs), 20 g (medium–large dogs), 18 g (large dogs/surgical per order); T-port or injection cap (primed with saline); saline flushes; clippers with clean blade; chlorhexidine or povidone scrub + alcohol per hospital protocol; 1/2–1" white tape pre-torn strips + vetwrap; gauze; tourniquet or holder occlusion; sharps container in reach; catheter label (date, gauge, initials).

## 2. Patient considerations
Cats: shorter catheter stick-out, kink risk at carpus — tape carefully, consider light splint per policy. Chondrodystrophic breeds: short forelimbs, prominent skin folds — stretch skin well. Dehydrated: pre-warm limb, expect slow flash. Cardiac/fluid-overload-risk patients: never open a line to gravity while securing. Fractious/painful: placement is elective until safety is planned.

## 3. Relevant anatomy
Same vessel as TS-VEN-CEPH-001; catheters ideally enter distally (dorsal carpus region upward) so a blown attempt still leaves proximal vein usable — **always start distal, move proximal on retries**.

## 4. Relevant physiology
The plastic catheter, not the stylet, must reside in the lumen: flash confirms stylet entry only; the catheter tip sits ~1–2 mm behind the stylet tip, so advance the unit slightly after flash before threading, or the catheter pushes against the outer wall and kinks.

## 5. Indications
DVM/CVT-ordered venous access: fluids, IV meds by authorized staff, surgery, emergencies.

## 6. Contraindications / do not proceed
Skin infection/burns/wounds at site; that limb reserved (surgeon preference, lymphatic concern); suspected coagulopathy without DVM direction; patient status changed since order (report first); your 2-attempt limit reached.

## 7. Preparation
Verify order (gauge, purpose, which limb if specified) and patient ID; prime T-port/cap; tear tape strips onto table edge in placement order; clip generously (carpal to mid-antebrachium, full circumference per hospital protocol); aseptic prep: scrub–alcohol alternating per protocol (contact time per hospital cleaning SOP); do not touch prepped entry point afterward — repalpate proximal/lateral to it or re-prep.

## 8. Step-by-step procedure
1. ID check aloud; holder occludes and rotates vein as for venipuncture, limb extended, carpus flexed slightly downward by your non-dominant hand applying distal traction.
2. Inspect catheter: slide catheter off/on stylet once to break seal; check tip integrity; never touch the shaft.
3. Bevel up, 15–25°, enter skin slightly distal-lateral to vein, then vessel: watch for flash in the hub chamber.
4. On flash: **drop angle flat, advance entire unit 1–2 mm** (seats catheter tip in lumen).
5. Hold stylet dead still; slide catheter forward off the stylet smoothly to the hub. It should glide without force. NEVER pull the catheter back over the stylet and re-advance (shear risk — catheter fragment embolism); if you must withdraw, remove catheter and stylet together as a unit.
6. Holder releases occlusion; occlude vessel over catheter tip with your thumb; withdraw stylet fully → directly into sharps.
7. Attach primed T-port/cap; flush 1–3 mL watching/palpating for a smooth flush with no subcutaneous bleb, no resistance, and (ideally) visible flash on aspiration.
8. Tape: strip 1 sticky-side-up under hub, crossed over; strip 2 flat over hub wings; strip 3 securing T-port loop; snug, not circumferentially tight — must slide one finger under.
9. Vetwrap light cover; label with date/time, gauge, initials.
10. Recheck flush after wrap (taping can kink); chart placement.

## 9. Safety precautions
Stylet is a contaminated sharp the instant it exits — straight to sharps, never on the table. Watch patient's mouth-end; a securing struggle is a stop point. Never leave an un-capped catheter open.

## 10. Common mistakes
Threading immediately at first flash without advancing the unit (catheter tip outside vessel → sub-q fluids later); too-steep entry through back wall; starting proximal (burns usable vein); insufficient clip/prep; tape too tight (limb swelling below wrap — the "fat paw"); tape too loose (catheter walks out); not breaking catheter-stylet seal first; flushing against resistance (dislodges clot or blows infiltrated fluid sub-q).

## 11. Failure modes & 12. Troubleshooting
No flash → slow withdrawal to skin, re-palpate, one redirect; else new attempt distal→proximal on other limb or hand off. Flash then can't thread → advance unit 1 mm more, drop angle, slight catheter rotation while threading; if still resisting, vein likely blown or valve — remove as unit, pressure, new site. Flash lost after advance → through back wall: remove as unit, pressure 60 s, next site proximal or other limb. Threads but won't flush / bleb forms → infiltrated: remove, pressure, report, new site. Kinks at carpus (cats) → re-tape with carpus in neutral, per-policy splint.

## 13. Patient complications
Hematoma; infiltration/perivascular swelling; phlebitis over time (heat, pain, cord-like vessel — report, expect removal order); catheter embolism (from re-threading over stylet — prevention only, escalate STAT if suspected); tourniquet-effect swelling from wraps.

## 14. Equipment complications
Burred catheter tip (won't thread — replace); hub crack (leaks at cap); T-port air (prime fully).

## 15. Stop criteria
2 unsuccessful attempts by you → hand off. Any distress-escalation trigger as in TS-VEN-CEPH-001. Suspected catheter shear → stop everything, keep patient still, escalate immediately.

## 16. Escalation criteria
Veins exhausted/flat; patient needs sedation decision; any §13 complication; order-vs-patient mismatch (e.g., 18 g ordered for a 3 kg cat — query, don't improvise).

## 17. Cleanup
Sharps; clipper blade cleaned/disinfected; prep waste; table disinfected; restock catheters+tape.

## 18. Documentation
Chart: date/time, limb/site, gauge, attempts, flush quality, securing method, patient tolerance, initials. Catheter label on wrap. Case log entry.

## 23. Critical-fail behaviors
No patient ID check; re-advancing catheter over stylet after pullback (shear risk); stylet left on table/bedding; flushing against firm resistance; exceeding attempt limit; circumferential tight tape without recheck; failing to recognize obvious infiltration on flush.

## 24. Repetition requirements (rationale)
Pre-assessment: ≥10 model placements; ≥3 observed; ≥5 coached live placements ≥1 cat ≥1 dog. State 9→10: ≥12 logged successful placements, ≥75% ≤2-attempt success, ≥3 patient categories. Rationale: threading mechanics and securing quality vary hugely with limb conformation; consistency across conformations is the competency. Educational requirement, not legal rule.
