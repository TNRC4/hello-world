# TS-VEN-CEPH-001 — Cephalic Venipuncture (Canine & Feline)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| School / Level | Core Academy / VA2 |
| Prerequisites | TS-RES-003 (positioning) ≥8; TS-ASM-001 ≥8; W3-01 vascular anatomy passed; TS-SAM-001 theory complete |
| Scope class / Supervision | P / S2 after sign-off (first 10 post-sign-off draws S1 per CVAH policy); scope row SCOPE-003 |
| Revalidation | 24 months, or after 6 months without logged draws (decay rule) |

## 1. Equipment
Needles 22–25 g (25 g for cats/small dogs; 22 g standard canine); syringes 1–6 mL matched to requested volume; butterfly (winged infusion) set 23–25 g for small/wiggly patients or fragile veins; collection tubes per test (see TS-SAM-001); alcohol 70% and gauze; clippers if coat obscures the vein (owner-consent awareness for show animals); sharps container within arm's reach; label materials.

## 2. Patient considerations
Cats: shorter, more mobile vein; lower tolerance for prolonged restraint — plan for a fast, quiet draw; consider medial saphenous alternative early. Small/pediatric: total safe sample volume matters — confirm requested volume is appropriate before sticking (flag to CVT if >1% body weight in mL is requested). Dehydrated/hypovolemic: flat, slow-filling veins — expect slow flow, use smaller syringe to reduce collapse. Geriatric/fragile: minimal negative pressure, expect rolling veins. Brachycephalic and respiratory-compromised: monitor breathing during restraint. Fractious: do not escalate restraint to force a cephalic — change plan (site, sedation per DVM, second team).

## 3. Relevant anatomy
The cephalic vein runs along the dorsomedial (cranial) surface of the antebrachium from the paw toward the elbow, superficial and palpable when occluded proximally at the elbow. The accessory cephalic joins distally on the dorsum — a usable Y in some dogs. Median nerve/artery lie deeper and medial; correct superficial technique avoids them. Skin, subcutis, then vein wall: two "gives" may be felt in thick-skinned dogs.

## 4. Relevant physiology
Occlusion impedes venous return → distension. Excess negative pressure collapses the vein and lyses cells (hemolysis). Prolonged occlusion (>1–2 min) causes hemoconcentration and can skew PCV/TS and potassium. Trauma/slow flow activates clotting — fill anticoagulant tubes promptly and mix gently.

## 5. Indications
Routine blood collection for in-house or reference lab testing when a DVM/CVT has ordered the sample; small–moderate volumes (jugular preferred for large volumes/blood culture).

## 6. Contraindications / do not proceed
That limb has, or is planned for, an IV catheter (never draw proximal to or from a catheterized limb without CVT/DVM direction); skin infection/wound over the site; lymphedema or post-mastectomy-style limb concern; patient decompensating or dyspneic in restraint; you are not confident in your restraint team; two failed attempts already made by you.

## 7. Preparation
Verify order and patient identity (two identifiers: name+client name, or ID band); assemble everything BEFORE the patient is positioned — tubes in fill order, needle seated, plunger broken loose; confirm holder knows the position (TS-RES-003); choose limb (avoid catheter-planned limb — ask surgery team if unsure); clip only if needed.

## 8. Step-by-step procedure
1. Confirm patient ID aloud with holder; state test and volume.
2. Position: sternal or sitting; holder extends limb, occludes at elbow with thumb rolling laterally to stabilize the vein.
3. Palpate the raised vein — trust feel over sight; note direction and depth. Alcohol swab (parts hair, improves visibility; allow a moment so it doesn't sting broken skin).
4. Stabilize distally with your non-dominant hand (thumb alongside — not on top of — the vessel below entry point, applying gentle distal traction).
5. Bevel up, 15–30° angle, enter skin and vein in one or two controlled motions, 0.5–1 cm distal to intended vessel entry for a shallow tunnel.
6. Watch hub for flash; on flash, drop angle nearly parallel and advance 1–2 mm to seat the bevel.
7. Aspirate gently — no more than ~0.5 mL of vacuum at a time; if flow stops, rotate needle slightly or ease off pressure before withdrawing.
8. On completion: holder releases occlusion FIRST, then withdraw needle, then immediate firm gauze pressure (assistant or holder) 30–60 s (longer if repeated attempts).
9. Transfer blood promptly: proper fill order, remove needle before injecting into tubes when using syringe (or use transfer device per hospital policy), let vacuum draw where applicable, gentle inversions per tube type (TS-SAM-001).
10. Needle to sharps immediately — never recap by hand.
11. Label at patient-side; check the site for hematoma before releasing patient; praise/decompress patient.

## 9. Safety precautions
Sharps: one-hand technique only; sharps container within reach before starting. Restraint welfare: continuous observation of breathing and stress. Never leave a needle unattended on the table with a patient.

## 10. Common mistakes
Occluding too briefly/too long; chasing a vein visually instead of palpating; entering too steeply (through-and-through); aspirating too hard (collapse + hemolysis); releasing needle before releasing occlusion (hematoma); slow tube transfer (clotted EDTA); underfilled citrate tube; shaking instead of inverting; forgetting distal traction so the vein rolls.

## 11. Failure modes
No flash (missed lateral/deep); flash then stop (bevel against wall, vein collapsed, through-back-wall); blood stops mid-draw (collapse, clot in needle, position shift); hematoma ballooning (back wall punctured or occlusion still on during withdrawal).

## 12. Troubleshooting
No flash → withdraw to just under skin, re-palpate, redirect once (a "redirect" without full exit counts as the same attempt; full exit = new attempt). Flash-then-stop → rotate bevel 90°, drop angle, advance 1 mm, ease aspiration; if still nothing, release/reapply occlusion. Slow flow in small patient → smaller syringe, butterfly set, warm compress on limb 30–60 s. Rolling vein → more distal traction, enter at slight lateral angle to pin vessel.

## 13. Patient complications
Hematoma (most common — pressure, note in record); bruising in coagulopathic patients (STOP, escalate — jugular absolutely contraindicated); stress escalation (see stop criteria); vasovagal-appearing collapse (rare — lay patient down, alert CVT/DVM immediately); nerve irritation (vocalization/pain out of proportion — withdraw, note site).

## 14. Equipment complications
Needle burr (snagging on entry — replace); syringe seal failure (air ingress, poor aspiration); tube vacuum loss (short fill — replace tube).

## 15. Stop criteria (hard rules)
STOP after **2 unsuccessful attempts by you** (exit-skin count) → hand to CVT/senior. STOP immediately if: patient distress escalates beyond moderate (struggling that restraint cannot safely absorb, open-mouth breathing in a cat, cyanosis, collapse); hematoma forms; holder calls a stop (anyone can call a stop — no rank in stop-calls); patient is dyspneic in the required position.

## 16. Escalation criteria
Report to CVT/DVM before further attempts when: veins flat/unrecoverable, suspected coagulopathy, sample volume seems inappropriate for patient size, patient repeatedly fractious (sedation decision = DVM), any complication in §13.

## 17. Cleanup
Sharps disposed; gauze/alcohol waste out; table disinfected (TS-SAN-001); clipper blades cleaned if used; restock used items.

## 18. Documentation
Record: date/time, site and side, needle gauge, attempts, volume, tubes collected, complications (or "none"), initials, and patient tolerance note. Case log entry per `16_Case_Logs/`.

## 23. Critical-fail behaviors (end attempt, automatic fail)
Wrong patient / no ID check; recapping by hand or unsafe sharp handling; exceeding 2-attempt limit without handing off; continuing through defined stop criteria; leaving occlusion on during withdrawal causing preventable hematoma AND failing to recognize it; failure to label at patient-side.

## 24. Repetition requirements (educational rationale)
Before assessment: ≥10 model sticks; ≥3 observed live draws; ≥5 coached successful live draws including ≥1 cat and ≥1 dog. For state 9→10: ≥15 logged successful draws with ≥80% first-or-second-attempt success across ≥2 species and ≥1 "difficult" flag (small/dehydrated/geriatric). Rationale: motor consistency across patient variation is the competency — single success demonstrates luck, not reliability. These are educational requirements, not legal rules.
