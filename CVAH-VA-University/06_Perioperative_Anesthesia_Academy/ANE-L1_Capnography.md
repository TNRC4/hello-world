# ANE-L1 — Capnography: The Best Friend You Have to Learn to Read

**Why this matters:** Capnography is the earliest reliable window on ventilation, circulation, AND equipment — one waveform watches three systems. It is also the monitor most likely to be ignored as "that squiggle."

**Objectives:** name the four phases of a normal capnogram; tie EtCO2 to ventilation and perfusion; recognize the seven teaching patterns; run the artifact-vs-real check sequence; describe any capnogram in words on a report.

**Core instruction:** **What it measures:** CO2 concentration at the sampling point across the breath. The normal capnogram: Phase I baseline (inspired gas, should read zero) → Phase II sharp upstroke (dead-space gas washing out into alveolar gas) → Phase III near-flat alveolar plateau (slight upslope normal) → EtCO2 = plateau's end value → Phase 0 steep downstroke on inspiration. **The three-system logic:** CO2 must be produced (metabolism), delivered to lungs (circulation), and exhaled (ventilation + circuit). So: EtCO2 climbing with steady breathing = ventilation inadequate for production; EtCO2 collapsing with unchanged ventilation = perfusion collapsing OR sampling interrupted — both emergencies to escalate; baseline lifting off zero = the machine is feeding CO2 back (rebreathing).

**The seven teaching patterns (waveform library INT-A3, 12 tracings):** 1) Normal box. 2) Sudden zero/flat — disconnection, extubation, total obstruction, arrest: hands on patient + circuit NOW. 3) Progressive baseline rise — rebreathing: absorbent/valves/fresh-gas. 4) Shark fin (slurred upstroke, sloped plateau) — expiratory obstruction: tube kink/secretions/bronchospasm-pattern. 5) Falling EtCO2, waveform intact — hyperventilation vs falling perfusion vs hypothermia drift: cross-check BP/HR/temp. 6) Rising EtCO2, waveform intact — hypoventilation (common, drug-depth-related) vs rare hypermetabolic states. 7) Curare cleft/oscillations & cardiogenic wiggle — awareness-level: describe, don't over-read.

**Artifact-vs-real sequence (drilled):** sampling line connected/kinked/water-trapped? → patient breathing (chest/bag)? → other parameters agree? → THEN interpretation — and the report happens alongside the check, not after: "EtCO2 just flatlined; checking circuit — bag isn't moving either" is a perfect sentence.

**What doesn't look right (drill set):** plateau reads 48 and climbing over 20 min on a 12 rpm ventilated patient (hypoventilation trend — report); baseline 4 mmHg (rebreathing — absorbent check); pristine numbers but you can't see the trace the machine claims (dampened line — water trap).

**Knowledge check:** 1) EtCO2 34→18 over two minutes, RR unchanged, then BP unreadable. System failing? (*Perfusion — this is a peri-arrest pattern; escalate as emergency.*) 2) Baseline won't touch zero. (*Rebreathing — absorbent exhaustion/valve fault/fresh-gas issue; report for anesthetist action.*) 3) Shark fin appears in a bulldog. First physical checks? (*Tube kink/position/secretions; circuit; report with waveform described.*)
