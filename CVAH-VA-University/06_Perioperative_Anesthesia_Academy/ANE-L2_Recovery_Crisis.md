# ANE-L2 — Recovery Vigilance & the Crisis Catalogue

**Why this matters:** The perioperative mortality literature keeps saying the same thing: a disproportionate share of anesthetic deaths happen in recovery, when monitoring effort collapses. The Academy graduate is the person who never lets that happen at CVAH.

**Objectives:** run specialty-level recovery monitoring; describe extubation-readiness observations; execute species/conformation protocols; for each catalogue crisis, state the recognition picture and YOUR role-specific response.

**Core instruction — recovery:** Staffing doctrine: an extubation-adjacent patient is a 1:1 assignment until protocol criteria met (swallowing returned + sustained, maintaining airway, moving purposefully, temp trending up past house threshold, pain addressed per DVM). Your observation loop (q5 early): airway sounds (silence is golden; stridor/stertor = act), breathing pattern/effort, MM color, pulse, temp, mentation trajectory, incision/bandage first-look. Feline recoveries: quiet, dim, hide-option; watch the "flip-flail" emergence — pad the space, hands off unless unsafe, report dysphoria-pattern for the anesthetist's call. Brachycephalic protocol: sternal head-up positioning, tongue out, tube stays in LATE per anesthetist, oxygen + re-intubation kit AT the kennel, extended watch (obstruction risk persists an hour+), never left alone early. Regurgitation: head down/side per direction, suction, report immediately (aspiration stakes).

**The crisis catalogue (recognition picture → your role):** hypotension (trend + pale MM → report trend early, warm, verify cuff) · hypertension (verify cuff/site, report — depth/pain question is anesthetist's) · bradycardia (cross-check pulse real vs ECG artifact, temp, report — alpha-2 context noted) · tachycardia (pain/depth/volume questions belong upstairs; your job: verified numbers + trend fast) · apnea (bag/chest silent → announce immediately, hands-on check, be ready to support as directed) · hypoventilation/hypercapnia (EtCO2 story per ANE-L1) · hypoxemia (SpO2 with waveform credibility check + MM color; announce, don't tinker silently) · airway obstruction (effort WITHOUT air movement = the scariest picture in the building — announce/act per direction instantly) · circuit disconnection (flat capnogram + limp bag) · rebreathing (baseline lift) · O2 supply failure (line pressure alarms — know the backup cylinder location/valve) · hypothermia (the quiet multiplier — trend it, warm it, report it) · depth extremes (light: reflex return, movement, HR/BP climb — announce before the patient moves; deep: reflexes gone, jaw slack, pressures sagging — announce) · arrest (recognition → RECOVER activation, your trained BLS role).

**Knowledge check:** 1) Recovery brachycephalic pulls the tube himself at minute 2. (*1:1 continues with heightened watch: positioning, O2 ready, anesthetist informed — self-extubation ≠ ready.*) 2) "Effort without air movement" means? (*Obstruction — the announce-now picture.*) 3) Bradycardia on the monitor: your two verifications while reporting? (*Palpated pulse agreement + temperature; ECG artifact and hypothermia both masquerade.*)
