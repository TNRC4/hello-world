# ANE-L4 — ECG & Blood Pressure: Clinically Useful, Honestly Messy
Tier T3 course · ~35 min + strip lab · Not a cardiology course; not "the line shows heart rate" either.

## PART A — ECG for the perioperative monitor
**What it is:** three electrodes reading the heart's ELECTRICAL sequence. P (atria fire) → QRS (ventricles fire) → T (reset). Electricity is not squeeze: the screen can look organized while the pump fails — your finger on a pulse is the mechanical half of every ECG observation. Setup that prevents most "arrhythmias": correct limb placement per house convention, fresh gel/alcohol contact, cables routed off the surgical field and away from moving drapes, patient not shivering on a metal table edge.
**The working recognition set (strips in INT-A4, 20 examples each class):**
1. *Normal sinus + sinus arrhythmia* (rate varies with breathing — normal in dogs; know its look so it stops scaring you).
2. *Sinus bradycardia* — slow but organized; perioperative context: drugs (alpha-2s, opioids), deepening anesthesia, hypothermia, or vagal events; your move: pulse-verify, temp-check, report with trend + drug context.
3. *Sinus tachycardia* — fast but organized; context: light plane, pain response, hypotension compensation, hypovolemia; your move: cross-check BP and depth signs, report the cluster.
4. *VPCs* (ventricular premature complexes) — wide, bizarre, early beats interrupting rhythm; occasional singles vs runs; your move: count them per minute, pulse-check (do they perfuse?), report — RUNS or multiform = report-now.
5. *Second-degree AV block pattern* — P waves marching, occasional QRS dropped; common with alpha-2s; report with drug context.
6. *Artifact zoo* — loose lead (chaotic spikes, patient fine), 60-cycle interference (fuzzy regular band), shiver/movement (irregular baseline noise), clipper interference. Rule: artifact is EXPLAINED (found and fixed), never assumed.
**The two mechanical cross-checks that make ECG useful:** every rhythm observation pairs with (a) palpated pulse — rate match? deficits? and (b) the pleth/BP — is electricity producing pressure? "ECG 140 organized, femoral 90 and thready" is a report-NOW sentence — that mismatch is the finding.

## PART B — Blood pressure with the messiness left in
**Doppler:** crystal over an artery (palmar/plantar/coccygeal), gel, headphones/speaker; cuff proximal, inflate till the whoosh vanishes, bleed down till it returns = systolic estimate. Strengths: works on small/arrhythmic patients, gives your EARS a continuous pulse-quality monitor. Honest limits: one number (systolic-ish; in cats it reads closer to mean), operator-dependent crystal placement.
**Oscillometric:** cuff auto-cycles, machine reports S/D/MAP. Strengths: hands-free trending. Honest limits: motion-intolerant, unreliable at extremes (small patients, arrhythmias, vasoconstriction) — precisely the patients that matter most.
**Cuff science (owns half of all BP errors):** width ≈ 40% of limb circumference — eyeball-check EVERY case; narrow reads HIGH, wide reads LOW; snug application (one finger); site level with the heart (a limb hanging below heart level reads high); same site+cuff for the whole case (trend integrity beats site-shopping).
**The trust algorithm (drilled):** a surprising number → (1) patient check: pulse feel, gums, pleth — do they corroborate? (2) technique check: cuff size/site/motion, repeat one cycle; (3) trend check: does it continue the story or teleport? THEN report with your verification attached: "MAP 52 twice, cuff correct, pulses gone soft — I believe it." vs "one reading of 40, patient pink, femoral strong, repeating — flagging early in case." Both are good reports; silent acceptance and silent dismissal are the two failure modes.
**Messy-case drills (in SIM library):** the cat whose oscillometric won't read (answer path: Doppler swap per direction, note method change on record); the Doppler that "lost pressure" when the limb slid off the pad (site-level physics); the trend that stair-steps down 8 mmHg per reading while everything else looks fine (believe trends — report before the cliff).

**Assessment:** strip-recognition set ≥85% (artifact items must be EXPLAINED, not just labeled); BP setup practical within PA-ANES-MON-001 (cuff-eyeball check verbalized ★); trust-algorithm scenario items in QB-ANES. *Reviews: R1 added the electricity-vs-squeeze pairing rule and cuff-level physics; R2 every waveform class has a look, a context, and a your-move; R3 hospital gains BP numbers with credibility scores attached — the thing DVMs actually need.*
