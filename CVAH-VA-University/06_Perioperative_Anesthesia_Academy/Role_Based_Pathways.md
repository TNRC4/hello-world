# Role-Based Perioperative Pathways & Competency Tiers

Version 1.0 · 2026-08 directive · Doctrine: **teach the medicine deeply, then separately define what each role may DO with it.** Role scope limits action, never understanding. The Academy serves advanced VAs AND credentialed technicians from one shared curriculum with layered application levels — a technician uncomfortable with anesthesia should leave this academy transformed; a competent technician should find meaningful advanced development, not beginner review (prerequisite testing routes them past mastered material).

## The three layers (applied per module)
**ADVANCED VA LAYER** — application focus: preparation, equipment mastery, patient observation, the five-beat monitoring loop, trend recognition, artifact discrimination, documentation, equipment troubleshooting, communication/escalation, recovery vigilance, anticipation. May physically check probes/leads/cuffs/circuit connections while reporting. May never: adjust vaporizer/APL/flows, administer/push drugs, intubate (CVAH policy), extubate, or make depth calls.
**TECHNICIAN DEVELOPMENT LAYER** — everything above plus deeper application within CVT scope and hospital protocol: integrated physiologic reasoning (why this hypotension, in this patient, on these drugs), anesthetic-depth assessment and adjustment under DVM direction, ventilation support, pharmacology at working depth (expected effects, interactions, reversal logic), advanced troubleshooting ownership (machine faults, difficult airway equipment prep), perioperative planning participation, crisis response roles per RECOVER ALS support. Delivered via the same modules' "technician extension" blocks + separate case discussions with the DVM.
**DVM-CONTROLLED LAYER** — explicitly named in every module: protocol selection, drug/dose decisions, depth prescriptions, intervention orders, crisis treatment decisions. The curriculum TEACHES why these decisions work (so monitoring anticipates them); it never delegates them.

## Perioperative competency tiers (promotion by demonstrated competency, never hours)
| Tier | Standard (all required) | Typical role |
|---|---|---|
| **T1 Perioperative Foundation** | Core graduate; CSC-SUR-01 signed; TS-SUR-STER-001 ≥9; sterile-conscience drill 100% catch; room setup/turnover practical | Prep-bay + circulator |
| **T2 Anesthesia Assistant** | T1 + TS-ANE-101 (machine check) signed incl. staged faults; airway kit staging drill; monitor placement (TS-ANE-102); induction/recovery support cases ×5 countersigned; QB-ANES equipment items ≥85% | Setup, induction support, recovery sitter under direction |
| **T3 Anesthesia Monitor** | T2 + CSC-ANE-01 mastery: PA-ANES-MON-001 passed; SIM-ANES library ≥85% incl. all "what doesn't fit" items; waveform set ≥85%; 15-case log with variety per Case_Log_Anesthesia schema; artifact-vs-real live drill | The trained second-eyes on every anesthetized patient |
| **T4 Advanced Perioperative (VA) / Technician Development (CVT)** | T3 + advanced sim set (crisis recognition) ≥85%; full perioperative practical (PA-PERIOP-EXAM); VA track: credential granted here. CVT track: adds technician-extension assessments (depth/ventilation/pharm application cases reviewed by DVM) per scope | VA: credentialed perioperative assistant. CVT: documented advanced development |

Prerequisite testing: any entrant may challenge T1–T2 content by exam + practical demonstration; experienced CVTs typically enter at T3 coursework directly. Nobody sits through mastered material; nobody skips unproven material.
