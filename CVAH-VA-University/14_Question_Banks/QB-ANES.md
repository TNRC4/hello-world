# QB-ANES — Perioperative & Anesthesia Academy (30 items)
Rewritten to Assessment_Item_Quality_Standard 2026-08. Flagship bank: distractor rationales included. Case values are teaching constructs; live thresholds per CRC-003.

### ANES-001 | ANE-M1 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Cardiac output is the product of:
A) Heart rate and blood pressure B) Heart rate and stroke volume C) Preload and afterload D) Stroke volume and vascular resistance
KEY: B. A is the everyday conflation (pressure is downstream of output AND resistance); C and D name real determinants of stroke volume/pressure but not the definition.

### ANES-002 | ANE-M1 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: As inhalant depth increases, blood pressure typically falls mainly because inhalants:
A) Slow the heart into profound bradycardia B) Shift blood into the splanchnic organs C) Vasodilate and depress contractility dose-dependently D) Suppress the kidneys' pressure regulation
KEY: C. A happens with some agents/adjuncts but isn't the primary inhalant mechanism; B and D are physiology-flavored stories. This is why depth and BP are read together.

### ANES-003 | ANE-M4 | D2 | L4 | SC:Y
Q: Mid-case, EtCO2 drops to near zero with a flat trace. SpO2 still reads 97%. The reassuring SpO2 means:
A) The problem is probably the capnograph B) Little — SpO2 lags on oxygen; check patient and circuit now C) Perfusion is confirmed adequate D) The sampling line is the likely fault, so no urgency
KEY: B. A and D both let one monitor excuse another — the flat capnogram is disconnection/obstruction/arrest until HANDS disprove it; C is exactly what SpO2 cannot promise during early apnea on oxygen.

### ANES-004 | ANE-M4 | D2 | L4 | SC:N
Q: The capnogram baseline has risen off zero over the last 20 minutes. The system is telling you:
A) The patient is hyperventilating B) The sampling line has water in it C) Inspired gas now contains CO2 — rebreathing D) Metabolic CO2 production is rising
KEY: C — exhausted absorbent, valve fault, or fresh-gas problem. A lowers EtCO2, not the baseline; B dampens the trace shape; D raises the plateau, not the floor.

### ANES-005 | ANE-M4 | D2 | L4 | SC:N
Q: A progressively slurred upstroke with a sloped plateau ("shark fin") most directly suggests:
A) Expiratory obstruction — kink, secretions, bronchoconstriction pattern B) Cuff leak — escaping gas distorting the sampled breath C) Cardiogenic oscillations transmitted from the heartbeat D) A reservoir bag oversized for the patient's tidal volume
KEY: A. B leaks show as low/irregular EtCO2 with normal shape early; C is a small-wave curiosity, not a fin; D affects bag feel, not waveform morphology.

### ANES-006 | ANE-M3 | D2 | L4 | SC:N
Q: SpO2 reads 89% with a clean, strong plethysmograph. Your read:
A) Artifact — probe cold or positional B) Recheck in five minutes before acting C) Credible desaturation — report now, describing the waveform D) The monitor needs a different probe site to confirm first
KEY: C — clean pleth is the credibility check passing. A ignores the evidence; B and D both delay on a number whose waveform says "believe me"; troubleshooting-first is for RAGGED waveforms.

### ANES-007 | ANE-M3 | D2 | L4 | SC:N
Q: SpO2 87%, pleth ragged since limb repositioning; MM pink, HR/EtCO2/BP stable. Best support action:
A) Report the value while repositioning the probe and rechecking color B) Silence the alarm — three parameters say the patient is fine C) Reposition first; report only if the new reading stays low D) Increase oxygen flow while you investigate
KEY: A — report AND test the artifact hypothesis simultaneously; never silently. B lets agreement erase a finding; C is the "silent troubleshooting" habit; D treats without reporting and isn't your dial anyway.

### ANES-008 | TS-ANE-101 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: The leak test passed. Before connecting a patient, the step that prevents the classic lethal error is:
A) Refilling the vaporizer to the line B) Recalibrating the oxygen flowmeter C) Swapping the reservoir bag for a fresh one D) Confirming aloud that the APL valve is open again
KEY: D — a closed pop-off after testing turns the circuit into a pressure trap. A, B, C are all plausible pre-case housekeeping with no bearing on the barotrauma path.

### ANES-009 | TS-ANE-101 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: Absorbent exhaustion shows most reliably as:
A) A violet color change alone B) A whistling sound on expiration C) The canister feeling cold to the touch D) Rising inspired CO2 on the capnograph
KEY: D — the monitor evidence. A is unreliable alone (color reverts on standing — the trap for visual-only checkers); C inverts the thermodynamics (active absorbent runs warm); B is not a described sign.

### ANES-010 | ANE-M2 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Non-rebreathing circuits suit small patients primarily because they:
A) Deliver anesthetic agent more precisely B) Need less oxygen flow per minute C) Minimize the resistance the patient breathes against D) Warm the inspired gases better
KEY: C. A is vaporizer physics, unchanged by circuit; B is backwards (they need MORE flow); D is also backwards (high fresh-gas flow is cold and dry).

### ANES-011 | ANE-M2 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: A reservoir bag far too large for the patient causes which practical problem?
A) It hides apnea and shallow breathing from your eyes B) It increases the patient's airway resistance C) It empties the oxygen supply faster D) It raises the risk of barotrauma directly
KEY: A — tiny excursions vanish in a huge bag; your best apnea monitor goes blind. B and D describe closed-APL and small-bag problems; C confuses bag volume with flow rate.

### ANES-012 | ANE-M3 | D2 | L4 | SC:Y
Q: Doppler in a cat reads 55 mmHg, down from 75 over 15 minutes. While reporting NOW you can usefully:
A) Recheck cuff size and position for the next reading B) Switch to the other limb and average the values C) Turn the vaporizer down half a percent D) Warm the limb to improve the signal
KEY: A — trend + verified technique is a complete report. B manufactures statistics from a deteriorating trend; C is not your dial, ever; D helps SIGNAL, not pressure, and delays nothing that matters here.

### ANES-013 | ANE-M3 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: A cuff much narrower than 40% of limb circumference will read:
A) Falsely low B) Accurately if centered well C) Falsely high D) Intermittently, dropping cycles
KEY: C — narrow cuffs need more pressure to occlude, inflating the number. A is the too-WIDE error (the paired fact); B wishes the problem away; D describes motion artifact.

### ANES-014 | ANE-M5 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: A dexmedetomidine-premedicated patient shows HR 55 with strong pulses and good pressure. Your read:
A) Bradycardia crisis — report as an emergency B) Expected alpha-2 pattern — note it, report per case thresholds C) Equipment artifact until proven otherwise D) Early anesthetic overdose
KEY: B — sedation with bradycardia and initial vasoconstriction is the drug's signature; monitoring anticipates it. A escalates a known pharmacologic pattern to crisis without checking the case's set bands; C invents artifact against physical evidence; D ignores the premed history.

### ANES-015 | ANE-M5 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: In premedication, opioids chiefly contribute:
A) Muscle paralysis for intubation B) Amnesia for the procedure C) Analgesia with sedation synergy D) Protection against arrhythmias
KEY: C. A describes neuromuscular blockers; B is a human-medicine benzodiazepine story; D is not an opioid role — respiratory depression is the watch-item instead.

### ANES-016 | ANE-M5 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: Which pairing of drug class → reversal agent is correct (educational level)?
A) Alpha-2 agonists → atipamezole B) Inhalants → naloxone C) Benzodiazepines → atropine D) Propofol → flumazenil
KEY: A. B pairs a real reversal with an unreversible class; C pairs two real drugs that have nothing to do with each other; D gives propofol the benzodiazepine antagonist. Knowing what CAN be reversed shapes crash-cart logic; administering is CVT/DVM.

### ANES-017 | ANE-M6 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: A gowned, gloved surgeon is sterile:
A) Front and back alike, from waist to shoulder line B) Front only, waist to shoulders, plus sleeves to cuff C) Everywhere the sterile gown physically covers D) The gloved hands and forearms only, nothing above
KEY: B. A grants the back sterility it never has (circulators route accordingly); C includes the below-waist gown; D forgets the gown front exists.

### ANES-018 | ANE-M7 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: The recovery patient needing the LONGEST extubation-adjacent watch:
A) A young greyhound after a lump removal B) A bulldog after routine anesthesia C) An adult DSH after a spay D) A geriatric lab after dentistry
KEY: B — brachycephalics obstruct AFTER the tube leaves, sometimes late; the watch extends an hour or more. A, C, D each carry real considerations (sighthound recovery, feline emergence, geriatric reserve) that don't approach the airway-collapse risk.

### ANES-019 | ANE-M7 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: During recovery positioning you notice fluid at the nares and a gulping motion. Sequence:
A) Alert the anesthetist now; head down/side per direction; suction ready B) Sit the patient sternal with the head raised high C) Wipe the nares and continue the recovery checks D) Offer a small amount of water to clear the throat
KEY: A — regurgitation near an unprotected airway is an aspiration setup. B raises the airway INTO the fluid path; C treats the evidence as mess; D adds material to the problem.

### ANES-020 | ANE-M4 | D3 | L5 | SC:Y
Q: Trend: EtCO2 58→64 over 15 min, RR 6, SpO2 96, BP stable. The coherent story is:
A) Hypoventilation — CO2 accumulating under a slow rate B) Equipment artifact — values this stable argue against patient change C) Malignant-hyperthermia-type CO2 surge D) Falling perfusion with preserved ventilation
KEY: A — slow rate + climbing CO2 + intact oxygenation on O2 is the classic picture; report for the anesthetist's ventilation decision. B misreads coherence as artifact; C ignores the modest slope and missing temperature; D would DROP EtCO2.

### ANES-021 | ANE-M4 | D3 | L5 | SC:Y
Q: Cat: HR 190, BP falling, EtCO2 falling, pleth weakening — all drifting together over 10 minutes. While reporting urgently, the most useful physical checks are:
A) Pulse palpation, MM/CRT, chest-and-bag movement B) Probe reposition and cuff resize C) ECG electrode contact and lead wires D) Vaporizer level and oxygen line seating
KEY: A — three independent monitors converging = patient problem until hands prove otherwise; your hands verify perfusion directly. B and C troubleshoot single-monitor stories against a multi-monitor consensus; D checks equipment that a falling-together pattern doesn't implicate first.

### ANES-022 | TS-ANE-103 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: Palpebral reflex has returned and jaw tone is rising mid-procedure. You:
A) Note it for the record at the next 5-min interval B) Ask the surgeon whether stimulation just increased C) Report it to the anesthetist immediately with the time D) Steady the patient's limbs in case of movement
KEY: C — lightening precedes movement; the anesthetist needs it NOW, not at the interval (A) and not after a side conversation (B). D prepares for the consequence instead of preventing it.

### ANES-023 | ANE-M2 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: The scavenging system's job is to:
A) Recycle unused anesthetic back to the vaporizer B) Reduce the oxygen bill C) Filter CO2 from the exhaled limb D) Carry waste anesthetic gas away from staff
KEY: D — occupational protection; checked at every setup. A and B describe machines that don't exist in practice; C is the absorbent's job.

### ANES-024 | ANE-M3 | D2 | L4 | SC:N
Q: ECG shows chaotic undulation. Pulse strong and regular at 110; SpO2 98 with clean pleth. Your action:
A) Announce possible arrest and start the arrest sequence B) Fix electrode contact and report what you found and did C) Ignore it — two monitors outvote one D) Print a strip and file it for rounds
KEY: B — mechanical evidence (pulse, pleth) says perfusion is real; dry clips/movement artifact is the likely story, but the finding-plus-fix still gets reported. A ignores the pulse in your fingers; C fixes nothing and reports nothing; D archives a live question.

### ANES-025 | TS-ANE-101 | D1 | L1 | SC:N
Q: Leak tests are performed:
A) Weekly, and after service visits B) Before every case and after circuit changes C) Daily, on the first machine start D) Monthly per the maintenance log
KEY: B. A, C, D are all real maintenance cadences for OTHER tasks — the leak test is per-case because circuits change per-case.

### ANES-026 | ANE-M7 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Extubation timing is governed by:
A) Airway reflex return, judged by the anesthetist B) A standard 15 minutes after vaporizer-off C) The patient lifting its head D) The recovery area being ready
KEY: A. B substitutes a clock for reflexes; C is one sign, late and unreliable alone; D is logistics. Your role: watch for swallowing and report it.

### ANES-027 | ANE-M4 | D3 | L4 | SC:N
Q: EtCO2 trace vanishes; SpO2 remains 98%. Ten seconds in, which interpretation ranks the possibilities correctly?
A) SpO2 is current, so the airway must be intact — check the capnograph line at leisure B) Both monitors are unreliable during surgery — go by MM color alone C) The capnograph fails more often than airways — replace the sampling line first D) The flat trace leads: check patient and circuit now; SpO2 stays reassuring for minutes during apnea on oxygen
KEY: D. A and C both let the lagging monitor and base rates excuse skipping the hands-on check; B abandons instrumentation instead of ranking it.

### ANES-028 | ANE-M6 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: A surgical count discrepancy at closing means:
A) Recount once, then proceed if the surgeon agrees B) Note it in the record for the practice manager C) Closure pauses until the discrepancy is resolved D) The circulator recounts alone while closure continues
KEY: C — counts stop closure; that is their entire authority. A, B, D each resume or delegate around the stop.

### ANES-029 | ANE-M1 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Equipment dead space matters most in:
A) Deep-chested large dogs with long airways B) Patients over 40 kg on rebreathing circuits C) Brachycephalic breeds with redundant pharyngeal tissue D) Very small patients, where adapters rival tidal volume
KEY: D — a few mL of adapter is trivial to a mastiff and a third of a kitten's breath. A, B, C attach the concept to size categories where it matters least or for other reasons.

### ANES-030 | ANE-M7 | D3 | L5 | SC:Y
Q: Recovery, 20 min post-extubation: RR climbing, visible effort, noisy inspiration, gums going dusky. You:
A) Escalate as an emergency; flow-by O2 while help comes; re-intubation kit at hand B) Reposition the head and neck, extend the tongue, recheck in five minutes C) Take a focused TPR so your report carries exact numbers and a trend D) Raise ambient oxygen near the kennel and reduce stimulation while watching
KEY: A — effort + noise + color change is post-extubation obstruction; minutes matter. B's repositioning is right but its five-minute wait is the killer; C measures while the airway closes; D is a treatment that doesn't exist.
