# QB-CORE-ASM — Patient Assessment, Inpatient, Communication (20 items)

### ASM-001 | TS-ASM-001 | D1 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Respiratory rate is counted:
A) Before handling, observed from a distance B) During gentle restraint for accuracy C) Right after the temperature, while hands are on D) Twice, and averaged with the stethoscope count
KEY: A — handling inflates the number; B, C, D all embed the artifact into the method.

### ASM-002 | TS-ASM-001 | D1 | L1 | SC:N
Q: One respiration equals:
A) Each visible chest movement B) One inhale plus one exhale C) One breath sound on auscultation D) One rise of the abdomen
KEY: B. A and D count half-cycles (the classic doubling error); C depends on where you listen.

### ASM-003 | TS-ASM-001 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: A pulse deficit is:
A) A femoral pulse that feels weak and thready under your fingers B) A pulse rate slower than the species' normal range C) A heartbeat heard without a matching femoral pulse D) A pulse palpable on one hindlimb but not the other
KEY: C — beats that don't perfuse; report it. A and B are quality/rate findings; D suggests your fingers, not the heart.

### ASM-004 | TS-ASM-002 | D1 | L1 | SC:N
Q: Normal capillary refill time:
A) Under 2 seconds B) 3–4 seconds C) 5 seconds if gums are pink D) Instantaneous only
KEY: A. B is abnormal; C lets color excuse timing; D over-tightens the standard.

### ASM-005 | TS-ASM-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: 9 AM temp on a hospitalized dog reads 105.2°F. You:
A) Recheck with a second thermometer at noon B) Begin cooling measures and document C) Chart it for morning rounds D) Report it now — out-of-band means at discovery
KEY: D. A delays behind fake rigor; B treats without an order; C is the "rounds will handle it" drift.

### ASM-006 | TS-ASM-004 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: The best-charted pain observation:
A) "Patient appears quite painful in the abdomen on my exam" B) "Tensed and vocalized on abdominal touch; pain score 3/4 per house scale" C) "Discomfort pattern suggestive of pancreatitis-type pain" D) "Somewhat uncomfortable earlier but appears to be settling now"
KEY: B — observable plus scaled. A concludes without evidence; C diagnoses; D editorializes.

### ASM-007 | TS-COM-001 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: The V in SBAR-V means the report ends with:
A) The key vitals restated as a closing summary B) Verbal confirmation of the response and next step C) A final verification of the patient's ID band D) A plan of action you volunteer to carry out
KEY: B — close the loop; an unacknowledged report is not a report. C recycles an earlier step; D exceeds role.

### ASM-008 | TS-INP-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: A treatment-sheet cell can't be completed — the patient left for imaging. Correct handling:
A) Escalate or annotate it so it is visibly owned B) Leave it blank; blanks show honesty C) Initial it now, do it when the patient returns D) Cross it out and write "at imaging"
KEY: A. B leaves an orphan no one owns; C is pre-charting; D removes the task instead of routing it.

### ASM-009 | TS-INP-001 | D3 | L4 | SC:Y
Q: Alert threshold HR 150. Your three checks read 118 → 129 → 141. You:
A) Recheck in an hour; the threshold hasn't been crossed B) Report the trend now C) Lower your own recheck interval and keep watching D) Note "HR climbing" for the shift handoff
KEY: B — trajectory beats snapshot. A and D wait for the number to finish the sentence; C watches deterioration in higher resolution.

### ASM-010 | TS-ASM-002 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: White-pale gums on a "stable" ward patient rate which urgency?
A) Recheck at the next scheduled round B) Note-and-monitor with food offered C) Report-now D) Report at rounds with the trend sheet
KEY: C — perfusion red flag; the scheduled-care options are the trap.

### ASM-011 | W2-02 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: Mid-history, an owner mentions their male cat strained in the litterbox all night with nothing produced. You:
A) Note it and continue — the history frame matters B) Alert the DVM/CVT immediately; possible obstruction C) Ask more urinary questions to firm up the picture D) Move the appointment to the top of the doctor's list
KEY: B — time-critical pattern; A and C keep interviewing an emergency; D routes it like a scheduling issue.

### ASM-012 | W2-02 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: The best appetite question:
A) "He's been eating fine this week, right?" B) "Have you noticed any big changes in his appetite lately?" C) "Tell me about his eating — how much of yesterday's meals did he finish?" D) "Has he always been a picky eater, or is that new for him?"
KEY: C — open, then quantified. A leads; B invites a shrug; D collects personality, not data.

### ASM-013 | TS-ASM-005 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: Tacky gums and a slow skin tent should be charted as:
A) "Dehydrated, approximately 8% by my assessment" B) "Tacky gums, delayed skin tent" — observations, reported C) "Dry — recommend starting fluids this morning" D) "Mildly dry mucous membranes, likely nothing significant"
KEY: B. A assigns the percentage (DVM's estimate); C prescribes; D interprets away.

### ASM-014 | TS-INP-103 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: A complete handoff includes, per patient:
A) Status trend, what's due next, watch-fors, lines/tubes — with read-back B) The diagnosis, the prognosis, and the doctor's current plan C) Anything unusual from the shift, told once, kept efficient D) The full chart and treatment sheet read aloud together
KEY: A. B is the DVM's layer; C has no read-back and no structure; D buries signal in noise.

### ASM-015 | TS-ASM-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: Purring cat — you can't isolate the heart rate. Best note:
A) Your closest auscultation estimate, clearly marked "approx" B) "HR unreliable — purring; femoral pulse strong and regular"; retry later C) The purr vibration rate, noted as an indirect proxy D) An empty HR field with a verbal note to the next shift
KEY: B — an honest limitation with collateral data beats a guess (A), a fiction (C), or an orphan blank (D).

### ASM-016 | W6-02 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: High-pitched inspiratory noise 10 minutes after extubation means:
A) Normal emergence vocalization B) Pharyngeal drying — offer a small water amount C) Stridor — CVT/DVM now D) The patient is too warm
KEY: C — airway narrowing; the re-intubation kit exists for this. A normalizes it; B feeds an unprotected airway; D is unrelated.

### ASM-017 | W6-02 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Recovery mentation should:
A) Trend steadily brighter; a plateau or dip is a report B) Cycle up and down as drugs redistribute C) Stay quiet — sleep is healing D) Match the pre-op baseline within 15 minutes
KEY: A. B and C excuse the dip; D sets an unrealistic clock that causes false alarms.

### ASM-018 | TS-DOC-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: You watched a treatment not happen, but it's charted as done. You:
A) Correct the chart yourself with an addendum B) Report it to the lead or manager C) Ask the charter about it casually first D) Let it go once — everyone has bad days
KEY: B. A puts your name on someone else's fiction; C gives the record time to stay wrong; D is how cultures rot.

### ASM-019 | TS-ASM-003 | D1 | L1 | SC:N
Q: Every weight entry must include:
A) The time of day B) The scale used C) The unit — kg or lb D) Whether the patient was fasted
KEY: C — unit mix-ups are dosing hazards; the others are nice-to-have context.

### ASM-020 | TS-COM-001 | D3 | L4 | SC:N
Q: The strongest "A" (assessment-of-observations) line in an SBAR-V:
A) "She's breathing weird and I really don't like how she looks" B) "I think she's developing aspiration pneumonia from recovery" C) "Something changed in the last hour and it's worth your time" D) "RR 64 and shallow at rest, up from 28 an hour ago; visible belly effort"
KEY: D — numbers, trend, observation. A and C are honest but unusable; B diagnoses.
