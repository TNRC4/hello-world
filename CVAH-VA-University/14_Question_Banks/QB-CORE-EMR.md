# QB-CORE-EMR — Emergency Foundations (15 items)
Rewritten to Assessment_Item_Quality_Standard 2026-08.

### EMR-001 | W6-04 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: An open-mouth-breathing cat arrives at the front desk. First move:
A) Weight and TPR so the doctor has numbers B) Room the cat somewhere quiet and dim C) Activate emergency response and start low-stress oxygen D) Take a focused two-minute history
KEY: C. A, B, D are all good routine care — and routine is exactly what this patient cannot afford.

### EMR-002 | W6-04 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: Large deep-chested dog: unproductive retching, tightening drum-like belly. This is:
A) Gastritis pattern — note for the doctor's next opening B) GDV pattern — activate now C) Bloat from gulping food — walk it to encourage burping D) Nausea — withhold food and monitor an hour
KEY: B — minutes matter. A, C, D each supply the household explanation that costs the stomach its blood supply.

### EMR-003 | TS-EMR-001 | D1 | L1 | SC:N
Q: Assistant CPR roles are:
A) Compressor, recorder, runner B) Compressor, intubator, recorder C) Recorder, drug-drawer, runner D) Any role the lead assigns
KEY: A. B and C each slip one restricted task into the list; D sounds flexible but the lead cannot assign outside scope.

### EMR-004 | TS-ECC-102 | D1 | L1 | SC:Y
Q: RECOVER compression rate:
A) 60–80/min B) 80–100/min C) 100–120/min D) 130–150/min
KEY: C — with full recoil and 2-minute compressor swaps.

### EMR-005 | TS-ECC-102 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Compression-to-ventilation ratio for a NON-intubated patient:
A) Continuous compressions, breaths when convenient B) 15:1 C) 10:1 with a tight mask D) 30:2
KEY: D. A describes the intubated pattern missing its 10 breaths/min; B and C are plausible-sounding inventions.

### EMR-006 | TS-EMR-001 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Closed-loop communication means:
A) Only the lead speaks during a code B) Repeating back each instruction before acting C) Keeping voices low to reduce chaos D) Confirming tasks at the end of each cycle
KEY: B. A and C manage noise, not information; D batches confirmation too late to catch a misheard drug.

### EMR-007 | W6-04 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: During an active seizure your role is to:
A) Restrain the head so it can't strike the floor B) Pull the tongue forward to protect the airway C) Protect the surroundings, time it, keep hands clear of the mouth, get the DVM D) Wrap the patient in a towel to shorten the seizure
KEY: C. A and D add restraint to a thrashing patient; B is the classic bitten-hand myth.

### EMR-008 | W6-04 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: The classic observable shock set:
A) Brick-red gums, bounding pulses, heavy panting at rest B) Pale gums, CRT over 2 s, weak fast pulses, dull mentation C) Pink gums, slow strong pulse, deeply sleeping posture D) Icteric gums, elevated temperature, generalized trembling
KEY: B. A is the early-sepsis/heat pattern (a deliberate near-miss); C and D are assembled from other conditions.

### EMR-009 | TS-EMR-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: The crash-cart audit finds a missing ET tube size. You:
A) Restock and report before signing B) Sign, then email the manager about the gap C) Sign with a marginal note about the tube D) Skip the audit line and flag it verbally
KEY: A — a signed audit certifies completeness; B, C, D all put a signature (or silence) ahead of the fix.

### EMR-010 | W6-04 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: A toxin-exposure call is 10 minutes out. Your prep:
A) Look up the toxin and start planning decontamination B) Alert the DVM, stage per direction, tell the client to bring the package C) Draw up an emetic so it's ready D) Prepare activated charcoal per the usual dose
KEY: B. A researches instead of activating; C and D prepare treatments nobody has ordered for a toxin nobody has identified.

### EMR-011 | TS-ECC-102 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: You're compressing and feel your quality fading at 90 seconds. You:
A) Push through to the 2-minute mark silently B) Slow slightly to conserve energy C) Announce it early and swap at the cycle break D) Step off now and wave someone in
KEY: C. A and B quietly degrade output (leaning and slowing kill perfusion); D breaks the cycle structure the swap exists to protect.

### EMR-012 | W6-04 | D2 | L2 | SC:Y
Q: A correct emergency activation sounds like:
A) "Can someone come up front when free?" B) "DR. NOW — TREATMENT — HIT-BY-CAR, breathing bad" C) A quick text to the doctor on shift D) "Emergency!" called toward the treatment area
KEY: B — loud, specific, located. A and C are polite delays; D is loud but carries no information to act on.

### EMR-013 | TS-EMR-001 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: After a code, the crash cart is:
A) Restocked and re-sealed the same day, logged B) Closed as-is until the scheduled weekly audit C) Restocked by whoever used the last item D) Left open in treatment as a reminder
KEY: A. B leaves a gap until the calendar notices; C diffuses ownership; D is a visual aid, not readiness.

### EMR-014 | W6-04 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: Owner on the phone: male cat straining, crying, producing nothing since last night. Scheduling guidance:
A) First appointment tomorrow morning B) Same-day-now emergency triage C) Drop-off this afternoon for a urine check D) Trial of wet food and a call back tonight
KEY: B — obstruction until proven otherwise; A, C, D are the three tempos of the same fatal delay.

### EMR-015 | TS-ECC-102 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Full recoil between compressions matters because:
A) It gives the compressor a sustainable working rhythm B) It reduces the rib-fracture rate over long codes C) Chest refill IS the perfusion mechanism — leaning kills output D) It keeps compressions visible for the recorder's count
KEY: C. A, B, D each attach a minor side effect to the main physiologic point.
