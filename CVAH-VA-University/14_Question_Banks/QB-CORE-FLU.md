# QB-CORE-FLU — Fluids & Pumps (15 items)
Rewritten to Assessment_Item_Quality_Standard 2026-08.

### FLU-001 | TS-FLU-001 | D2 | L2 | SC:Y
Q: Free-flow boluses are prevented by:
A) Modern pumps' anti-free-flow doors B) Closing the roller clamp before the pump door opens C) Keeping the bag below heart level D) Priming with the chamber inverted
KEY: B — the habit, not the hardware. A is what everyone assumes until the one pump without it; C and D are fragments of other procedures.

### FLU-002 | TS-FLU-001 | D1 | L1 | SC:N
Q: The drip chamber is correctly filled:
A) Completely, to exclude air B) One-quarter, to maximize visible drops C) One-third to one-half D) Level doesn't matter with a pump
KEY: C. A blinds drop-counting; B risks air entrainment; D forgets gravity-fallback and bolus-on-inversion.

### FLU-003 | TS-FLU-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: 120 mL/h on a 10 gtt/mL set is:
A) 12 gtt/min B) 20 gtt/min C) 30 gtt/min D) 2 gtt/sec
KEY: B — 120/60 × 10. A divides by 10 instead; C uses a 15 gtt set's math; D is off by an order.

### FLU-004 | TS-FLU-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: A pre-mixed "+KCl 20 mEq" bag arrives for your patient. Your checks before hanging:
A) Verify against the order with the CVT; pump only; never free-flow B) Confirm the label and hang on gravity with a careful drip count C) Mix by inversion, confirm the label, and hang on the pump D) Weigh the bag against the expected volume before hanging it
KEY: A. B does real verification then chooses the one delivery method potassium forbids; C skips verification; D is theater.

### FLU-005 | TS-FLU-002 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: Air-in-line alarm. The forbidden response:
A) Clamp, disconnect at the port per protocol, re-prime the segment B) Replace the administration set entirely C) Open the pump door and flick the bubbles toward the patient D) Silence once while you fetch supplies to fix it
KEY: C — flicking bubbles patient-ward is the named sin. A and B are the correct family; D is acceptable only as prelude to an actual fix.

### FLU-006 | TS-FLU-001 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: The hourly line check is:
A) Pump display check with a quick glance at the site B) Full patient-to-bag trace, site palpation, rate-vs-bag reality, tangle audit C) Careful site palpation — the pump already watches the line for you D) Bag level, label, and remaining-volume math at the pole
KEY: B. A, C, D each trust one instrument to cover the others' blind spots.

### FLU-007 | TS-FLU-002 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: Occlusion alarm. First move:
A) Trace the line — kink, closed clamp, positional limb — then the site B) Silence it and raise the pressure limit one step C) Flush the catheter to clear a suspected clot D) Restart the pump and watch for recurrence
KEY: A. B treats the alarm as a nuisance; C flushes against a possible occlusion (the taught never); D reruns the experiment without looking.

### FLU-008 | TS-FLU-001 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: A cloudy fluid bag with an intact wrapper is:
A) Usable if it clears on warming B) Usable for line-flushing only C) Discarded and reported D) Returned to the warmer shelf
KEY: C — clarity is a release criterion. A and D coax it back into stock; B invents a second-tier use for a failed product.

### FLU-009 | TS-FLU-002 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: The syringe-pump menu asks for syringe brand and size because:
A) Billing and inventory track consumables by brand and size B) The pump calibrates plunger force by syringe model C) Latex-free brands need a different infusion setting D) The entry sets occlusion-alarm sensitivity for that syringe
KEY: B — wrong selection = wrong delivery. A, C, D are plausible database-ish reasons that miss the mechanism.

### FLU-010 | TS-FLU-001 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Air discipline in priming matters most for:
A) Large dogs on high rates B) Any patient on gravity flow C) The smallest patients D) Patients on additive fluids
KEY: C — champagne bubbles scale with patient size. A, B, D distribute the concern where it bites least.

### FLU-011 | TS-FLU-001 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Bag-change documentation must capture:
A) The new bag's lot number and expiration for traceability B) Volumes remaining and discarded, plus new-bag verification C) The pump's total-delivered reading at the moment of change D) The time of the change and your initials in the record
KEY: B — I/O math depends on it. A, C, D each record something real and insufficient.

### FLU-012 | TS-FLU-002 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: A pump alarms repeatedly; two minutes of checking finds nothing. You:
A) Keep silencing — some pumps are just talkative B) Move the pump to another patient to test it C) Escalate, swap per CVT, tag the pump out of service D) Turn it off and run the line by gravity at the same rate
KEY: C. A normalizes alarms; B experiments on a second patient; D silently converts a precision order to a drip guess.

### FLU-013 | TS-FLU-001 | D1 | L1 | SC:N
Q: The line label carries:
A) Date/time started, set type, initials B) The current fluid rate and any additives C) The ordering DVM's name and the order date D) The patient's name and morning weight
KEY: A — set-change schedules run on it; the rest live in the chart.

### FLU-014 | TS-FLU-001 | D2 | L1 | SC:N
Q: Pediatric/micro drip sets deliver:
A) 10 gtt/mL B) 15 gtt/mL C) 20 gtt/mL D) 60 gtt/mL
KEY: D — fine control for small patients; A and B are the macro sets, C is a human-medicine size.

### FLU-015 | TS-FLU-001 | D3 | L4 | SC:Y
Q: The pump claims 200 mL delivered; the bag has lost about 40 mL. The possibility to exclude FIRST:
A) Evaporative loss through the bag film B) A mis-loaded set delivering outside the pump's control C) A mislabeled bag volume from the supplier D) Someone else changed the bag without charting
KEY: B — a free path is a live patient-safety problem; stop, trace, report. A is physically negligible; C and D are bookkeeping explanations you check after the patient is safe.
