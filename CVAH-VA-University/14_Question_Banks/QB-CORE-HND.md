# QB-CORE-HND — Handling & Restraint (18 items)

### HND-001 | TS-HND-001 | D1 | L1 | SC:N
Q: The EARLY canine stress signals — the ones to name before escalation — include:
A) Growling and snapping B) Lip licking, yawning, looking away C) Piloerection and lunging D) Freezing with a hard stare
KEY: B. A and D are late-ladder; C mixes arousal signs; the point is catching the quiet rungs.

### HND-002 | TS-HND-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: Carrier cat: pupils blown, ears flat, silent. Best opening move:
A) Tilt the carrier gently so the cat slides forward B) Reach in low and slow with a calm voice C) Remove the carrier top with a towel ready, work in the bottom half D) Leave the room for five minutes to let the cat settle
KEY: C. A and B are the two classic extraction injuries waiting; D rarely helps a cat already in threat posture and stalls the visit without a plan.

### HND-003 | TS-RES-001 | D1 | L2 | SC:Y
Q: In lateral restraint, the forearm across the neck must never:
A) Rest with any weight at all B) Press on the trachea C) Cross the shoulder line D) Stay in one position
KEY: B. A overshoots into uselessness; C and D invent rules — the airway is the untouchable.

### HND-004 | TS-RES-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: Mid-restraint a cat begins open-mouth breathing. You:
A) Stop and release the position immediately, alert the CVT B) Loosen your grip and finish the last few seconds C) Switch to a lighter towel hold D) Pause, let the cat take a few breaths, resume gently
KEY: A. B, C, D are all graduated responses to what is not a graduated situation — feline open-mouth breathing is decompensation.

### HND-005 | TS-RES-002 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: The dog keeps sitting during standing restraint. The fix that works:
A) Lift the rear back up each time it drops B) Have a second person hold the tail head C) Switch to firm verbal corrections D) Support under the abdomen earlier in the hold
KEY: D — prevent, don't fight. A and B wrestle the symptom; C trains nothing mid-procedure.

### HND-006 | TS-RES-003 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: The phlebotomist says "release." You release:
A) The whole patient, gently B) Your restraint arm, keeping the occlusion C) The occlusion only, restraint continues D) Nothing until the needle is visibly out
KEY: C. B inverts the two jobs; A and D bracket the right answer with over- and under-release.

### HND-007 | TS-HND-003 | D2 | L2 | SC:Y
Q: Muzzles are contraindicated when the patient is:
A) A brachycephalic breed B) Vomiting or dyspneic C) Under 5 kg D) Already wearing an e-collar
KEY: B — airway and aspiration. A needs the right STYLE of muzzle, not none; C and D are logistics, not contraindications.

### HND-008 | TS-HND-001 | D1 | L1 | SC:N
Q: "Stop" during restraint may be called by:
A) The most senior person present B) The person holding the patient C) Anyone on the team D) The DVM or CVT only
KEY: C — no rank in stop-calls; every other option installs a hierarchy that delays stops.

### HND-009 | TS-RES-003 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: For a jugular hold, the head position is:
A) Raised gently, nose up, short of hyperextension B) Hyperextended for maximum groove exposure C) Turned 90° away from the phlebotomist D) Level, with the chin cupped downward
KEY: A. B trades airway for exposure; C twists the vessel path; D hides the groove entirely.

### HND-010 | TS-HND-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: Three people now lean on one struggling 8 kg dog. The correct read:
A) Add a towel over the eyes to cut the visual stimulation and continue B) The plan has failed — stop and replan; sedation is the DVM's conversation C) Rotate in a fresh, stronger holder while the position is held D) Move faster so the stressful part is over sooner for everyone
KEY: B. A, C, D all escalate force on a patient whose stress is already past the plan's capacity.

### HND-011 | TS-RES-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: Which patient must NOT go into full lateral without DVM input?
A) An obese beagle due for rads B) A dyspneic cat needing a sample C) An arthritic senior with a sore hip D) A wiggly puppy for a nail trim
KEY: B — recumbency costs ventilation. C needs side-selection care, not prohibition; A and D are routine.

### HND-012 | TS-HND-004 | D1 | L1 | SC:N
Q: The towel burrito's function:
A) Warmth and familiar scent to lower the cat's arousal B) A visual barrier so the cat cannot target approaching hands C) Containment of limbs and claws with minimal force, plus a hide option D) A secure package that makes the cat safer to lift and carry
KEY: C. A, B, D each name a minor side benefit as the purpose.

### HND-013 | TS-HND-005 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Before any leash or carrier barrier comes off, you:
A) Audit doors and escape routes B) Announce it to the room C) Get treats within reach D) Check the patient's ID band
KEY: A. B, C, D are all good habits that don't stop a bolting patient.

### HND-014 | TS-HND-001 | D3 | L4 | SC:N
Q: A record reads "bit without warning." The most professionally useful interpretation:
A) Some dogs genuinely give no readable signals before biting B) The warnings were likely missed — earlier records should capture observed signals C) The breed's bite threshold is simply lower than average D) The previous handler was careless and the record should say who
KEY: B — "no warning" almost always means unread rungs; the fix is signal-literate documentation. A and C excuse the miss; D turns a systems lesson into blame.

### HND-015 | TS-RES-002 | D1 | L1 | SC:N
Q: The default feline position for most procedures:
A) Full lateral with limbs gathered B) Gentle dorsal in a trough C) Standing with chin support D) Sternal "meatloaf" with minimal fixation
KEY: D — least restraint that achieves stillness; A, B, C each fit specific procedures, not the default.

### HND-016 | TS-HND-002 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: A cat is plastered at the back of the carrier at the exam-room door. You:
A) Take the carrier in, disassemble the top, towel ready B) Tip the carrier slightly and let gravity help C) Wait in the lobby until the cat relaxes D) Reach in wearing exam gloves for grip
KEY: A. B and D are extraction attempts with injury pedigrees; C stalls without changing anything the cat cares about.

### HND-017 | TS-RES-004 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: The chart flags "will bite — sedation protocol." The DVM is at lunch and the nail trim is quick. You:
A) Try calmly — flags often overstate B) Muzzle first, then trim fast C) Wait for the DVM and the flagged plan D) Have two strong holders make it safe
KEY: C — flags exist because someone already learned this lesson; A, B, D are the three standard ways it gets re-learned.

### HND-018 | TS-RES-003 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Correct cephalic occlusion is:
A) A firm squeeze around the mid-forearm B) Thumb pressure at the elbow crook with a slight outward roll C) Two fingers pinching over the vein itself D) Steady pressure at the carpus
KEY: B — occlude high, roll to present. A occludes everything and provokes; C blocks your target; D is below the site and does nothing.
