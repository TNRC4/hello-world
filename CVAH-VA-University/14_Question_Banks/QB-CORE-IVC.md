# QB-CORE-IVC — IV Catheterization (18 items)

### IVC-001 | TS-IVC-CEPH-001 | D1 | Recall | SC:Y
Q: After pulling a catheter partially back over its stylet, you may:
A) Re-advance it carefully B) Never re-advance — remove catheter and stylet together as a unit C) Flush first, then advance D) Rotate and advance
KEY: B — Re-advancing can shear the tip = catheter embolism.

### IVC-002 | TS-IVC-CEPH-001 | D2 | Apply | SC:N
Q: Flash appears. Before threading you:
A) Thread instantly B) Drop the angle and advance the whole unit 1–2 mm C) Withdraw slightly D) Release occlusion
KEY: B — Catheter tip trails the stylet; seat it in the lumen first.

### IVC-003 | TS-IVC-CEPH-001 | D1 | Recall | SC:N
Q: Site strategy for attempts is:
A) Proximal first B) Distal first, moving proximal on retries C) Random D) Same spot repeatedly
KEY: B — A blown distal site leaves vein above it usable.

### IVC-004 | TS-IVC-CEPH-001 | D2 | Apply | SC:Y
Q: The flush meets firm resistance. You:
A) Push harder B) Stop — never force; positional check, gentle aspirate, report C) Pull the catheter out yourself immediately D) Add heparin
KEY: B — Forcing can dislodge clot or blow infiltrated fluid sub-q.

### IVC-005 | TS-IVC-002 | D2 | Apply | SC:N
Q: Infiltration classically shows as:
A) Warm red cord B) Cool, doughy perivascular swelling, sluggish/failed flush C) Purring D) Bleeding at hub only
KEY: B — [DIST: A is the phlebitis pattern.]

### IVC-006 | TS-IVC-002 | D2 | Apply | SC:N
Q: Phlebitis pattern is:
A) Cool swelling B) Heat, redness tracking the vessel, cord-like feel, pain on flush C) Wet tape D) Kinked line
KEY: B — Report; expect a removal order.

### IVC-007 | TS-IVC-CEPH-001 | D2 | Apply | SC:N
Q: Correct tape tension test:
A) As tight as possible B) One finger slides under; snug not circumferential-tight C) Loose enough to rotate D) No tape, just vetwrap
KEY: B — The "fat paw" is a tourniquet injury.

### IVC-008 | TS-FLU-002 | D2 | Apply | SC:Y
Q: A silent pump proves:
A) The line is fine B) Nothing about where fluid is going — trace and palpate the site hourly C) The patient is hydrated D) The rate is right
KEY: B — Pumps count volume, not destination.

### IVC-009 | TS-IVC-CEPH-001 | D2 | Apply | SC:Y
Q: The used stylet goes:
A) On the table for now B) Directly into sharps at withdrawal C) In your pocket D) Back in the wrapper
KEY: B.

### IVC-010 | TS-IVC-CEPH-001 | D2 | Apply | SC:N
Q: Catheter won't thread despite flash; you've advanced and flattened. Next:
A) Force it B) Slight rotation while threading; if still resistant, remove as a unit and choose a new site C) Pull catheter back and re-advance D) Flush to open the way
KEY: B — [DIST: C is the shear scenario; D pushes fluid into tissue if not in lumen.]

### IVC-011 | TS-IVC-CEPH-001 | D1 | Recall | SC:N
Q: Catheter labels must carry:
A) Just tape B) Date/time, gauge, initials C) Patient's favorite treat D) Nothing if charted
KEY: B — Dwell time drives phlebitis surveillance.

### IVC-012 | TS-IVC-002 | D2 | Apply | SC:N
Q: Morning check: wrap wet, fur at hub damp, pump running "normal." You:
A) Dry it and move on B) Stop the pump, trace, assess site, report — likely leak/displacement C) Add more tape over it D) Increase the rate
KEY: B.

### IVC-013 | TS-IVC-CEPH-001 | D2 | Apply | SC:N
Q: An 18 g catheter is ordered for a 3 kg kitten. You:
A) Place it as ordered B) Query the order — probable mismatch; typical kitten gauge is 24–22 g C) Choose 24 g silently D) Skip the catheter
KEY: B — Ask, never improvise or silently deviate.
