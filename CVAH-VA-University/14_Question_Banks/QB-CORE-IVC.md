# QB-CORE-IVC — IV Catheterization (18 items)
Rewritten to Assessment_Item_Quality_Standard 2026-08. Flagship bank: distractor rationales included.

### IVC-001 | TS-IVC-CEPH-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: The catheter is halfway back over the stylet after a failed thread. Which options remain legal? (1) re-advance it carefully (2) remove both together as a unit (3) rotate, then re-advance (4) withdraw fully and start a new site.
A) 1 and 4 B) 2 and 4 C) 2 and 3 D) 1, 3, and 4
KEY: B. Any re-advance over the stylet — careful, rotated, or otherwise — can shear the tip into the vein (catheter embolism). The temptation in 1 and 3 is exactly what the rule exists to kill.

### IVC-002 | TS-IVC-CEPH-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: You see flash. What happens between flash and threading?
A) Drop the angle and advance the whole unit 1–2 mm B) Release occlusion so the vein relaxes C) Withdraw the stylet a few millimeters first D) Thread immediately while the flash is fresh
KEY: A — the catheter tip trails the stylet; seat it in the lumen first. D is the classic rookie move that leaves the catheter outside the vessel; C removes your rigidity before the catheter is in; B deflates the vein you're threading into.

### IVC-003 | TS-IVC-CEPH-001 | D1 | L2 | SC:N
Q: First attempt fails low on the limb. Where does attempt two go?
A) The same spot with a fresh catheter B) Distal on the other limb, or proximal on this one C) Proximal on this limb only — never switch limbs D) The jugular, since the cephalic is used
KEY: B — fail distal, move up (or restart distal on a fresh limb). A re-punctures traumatized vessel; C invents a rule that wastes the other limb; D escalates site class without escalating people.

### IVC-004 | TS-IVC-CEPH-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: The first flush meets firm resistance. Your thumb could overcome it. You:
A) Push through — new catheters often start stiff B) Pull the catheter out and re-prep the site C) Stop, check limb position, aspirate gently, report D) Switch to a smaller syringe for better feel
KEY: C. A can fire a clot or blow infiltrated fluid subcutaneously; B destroys a line that might be positional; D (smaller syringe = MORE pressure, not less) is a physics trap — higher force per area, worse choice for exactly this moment.

### IVC-005 | TS-IVC-002 | D2 | L4 | SC:N
Q: A catheterized limb is swollen, cool, and doughy below the wrap; the flush goes in but a soft bump rises at the site. This pattern is:
A) Phlebitis of the vessel — expect a removal order to follow B) Normal post-placement edema from the insertion trauma C) A tape reaction that needs re-wrapping with lighter tension D) Infiltration — the catheter is no longer delivering into the vein
KEY: D. A is the WARM, red, corded pattern — the discrimination this item exists to test; B normalizes a failure sign; C explains the swelling but not the bleb on flush.

### IVC-006 | TS-IVC-002 | D2 | L4 | SC:N
Q: Day 2 check: the vessel above the catheter is warm, reddened, and cord-like, and the patient flinches on flush. You report it expecting:
A) An order to slow the fluid rate B) An order to remove the catheter C) A warm compress order to ease the vessel D) An order to flush with heparinized saline
KEY: B — that's the phlebitis pattern and dwelling catheters with phlebitis come out. A, C, and D all "treat" while leaving the cause in the vein; they're plausible-sounding half-measures.

### IVC-007 | TS-IVC-CEPH-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: Which taping result is correct?
A) Snug, with one finger able to slide under the wrap B) Tight enough that the wrap cannot rotate at all C) Loose enough to slide the wrap up and down freely D) Tight at the hub, loose everywhere else
KEY: A. B is the tourniquet ("fat paw") error stated confidently; C walks out with the first shake; D concentrates a pressure ring exactly where swelling starts.

### IVC-008 | TS-FLU-002 | D2 | L4 | SC:Y
Q: A pump has run "smoothly" for 6 hours with no alarms. What does that tell you about the catheter site?
A) Nothing — pumps count volume, not destination B) The line is patent, or the pump would alarm C) The site is fine unless the patient chews the line D) Delivery is confirmed if totals match the bag
KEY: A. B and D describe what pumps SEEM to promise — occlusion alarms trigger on pressure, and subcutaneous space accepts fluid at normal pressures; C narrows the risk to the visible one. Hourly trace-and-palpate exists because of exactly this gap.

### IVC-009 | TS-IVC-CEPH-001 | D1 | L2 | SC:Y
Q: The stylet's journey after it leaves the catheter:
A) Onto the tray until the line is secured B) Held in your free hand until taping is done C) Back into its original packaging D) Directly into the sharps container
KEY: D. A and B are the real-world habits that end in stick injuries during the busiest 30 seconds of the placement; C recaps by another name.

### IVC-010 | TS-IVC-CEPH-001 | D2 | L4 | SC:N
Q: Flash was clean, you advanced and flattened, but threading still resists. Reasonable next move:
A) Flush while threading to float the catheter in B) Slight rotation while threading; if still stuck, out as a unit, new site C) Pull the catheter back a touch and re-advance D) Push the stylet deeper to regain the lumen
KEY: B. A pushes fluid into tissue if the tip is extraluminal; C is the shear setup; D likely exits the back wall — flash promised the stylet was in, not that it stayed in.

### IVC-011 | TS-IVC-CEPH-001 | D1 | L1 | SC:N
Q: The catheter label must show:
A) Gauge, limb, and the patient's weight B) Initials and the fluid type C) Date and the ordering doctor D) Date, time, gauge, and initials
KEY: D — dwell-time surveillance runs on date/time; accountability on initials; gauge for flow decisions. The others each mix in plausible chart data that belongs in the record, not on the wrap.

### IVC-012 | TS-IVC-002 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: Morning rounds: wrap damp, fur at the hub wet, pump display normal, site soft. Sequence?
A) Re-tape with fresh dry materials, note it in the chart B) Increase the rate briefly to confirm delivery C) Stop the pump, trace the line, assess the site, report D) Dry the area and recheck at the next scheduled round
KEY: C — wet at the hub means leak or displacement until proven otherwise, and the pump keeps pushing meanwhile. A re-dresses over an unexplained failure; B adds volume to an unknown destination; D schedules a problem instead of solving it.

### IVC-013 | TS-IVC-CEPH-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: The order says 18 g for a 3 kg kitten. You:
A) Place a 24 g — obviously what was meant B) Place the 18 g as written C) Query the writer before placing anything D) Ask another assistant which they'd use
KEY: C. A silently deviates from an order (right size, wrong process — the trap for confident learners); B follows an order into a wall; D outsources a verification that belongs with the order's author.

### IVC-014 | W4-01 | D1 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Flow through a catheter is dominated by:
A) The length of the extension set B) The fluid bag's height C) The internal radius — to the fourth power D) The stiffness of the catheter material
KEY: C. A and B are real but linear factors (the plausible near-misses); D matters for kinking, not flow physics.

### IVC-015 | TS-IVC-002 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: The strongest argument for an e-collar on a catheterized, line-chewing-history patient:
A) It reassures the client the hospital is careful B) It keeps the wrap dry during walks C) A chewed line at 3 AM is a bleeding, open-vein event D) It shortens the morning line-check routine
KEY: C — chewed lines and catheters are genuine emergencies. A, B, D are all mildly true and all miss the actual risk being managed.

### IVC-016 | TS-IVC-CEPH-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: Second attempt just failed. The patient is calm and the CVT is busy in imaging. You:
A) Wait for the CVT — the limit has been reached B) Take one more attempt while conditions are good C) Move to the saphenous and restart the count D) Warm the limb and retry in fifteen minutes
KEY: A. B, C, and D each construct a loophole (conditions, site, time) — the limit counts attempts by the person, not by site or clock, and "calm patient, busy senior" is precisely when loopholes get invented.

### IVC-017 | TS-IVC-CEPH-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: Standard site preparation before insertion:
A) Alcohol swab, then palpate with a clean finger and insert B) Generous clip, antiseptic cycles, then hands off the entry point C) Clip only — antiseptic stings and blows veins D) Antiseptic cycles over unclipped fur to save time
KEY: B. A undoes the prep with the palpating finger (the near-universal habit this item targets); C trades asepsis for comfort folklore; D leaves the pathogen coat in place and preps it politely.

### IVC-018 | TS-FLU-001 | D1 | L2 | SC:N
Q: The T-port stress loop exists so that:
A) A tug on the line pulls tape, not catheter B) Air bubbles collect away from the patient C) Injections reach the vein faster D) The wrap stays looser at the hub
KEY: A — strain relief. B, C, D each attach a function the loop doesn't perform but sounds like it might.
