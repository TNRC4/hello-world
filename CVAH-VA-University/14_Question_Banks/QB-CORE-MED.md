# QB-CORE-MED — Medications & Math (25 items)
Rewritten to Assessment_Item_Quality_Standard 2026-08. Flagship bank: distractor rationales included.

### MED-001 | TS-MED-001 | D1 | L1 | SC:Y
Q: The five rights are patient, drug, dose, route, and:
A) Temperature B) Time C) Technique D) Team member
KEY: B — plus documentation and response as the additional two. C and D sound procedural but aren't the canonical five; A belongs to storage, not administration.

### MED-002 | TS-MED-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: Two vials of the same drug: one 5 mg/mL, one 50 mg/mL. This shelf situation is dangerous chiefly because:
A) The stronger vial expires faster B) One is probably a compounded product C) A concentration misread changes the dose tenfold D) The two labels come from different brands
KEY: C. A, B, D are all shelf-management concerns a learner might raise — none is the tenfold-overdose mechanism the concentration line carries.

### MED-003 | TS-MED-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: An unlabeled filled syringe sits on the treatment counter. A coworker says "that's Bella's cerenia, I just drew it." You:
A) Administer it — a direct witness identified it B) Label it now with what the coworker said C) Ask the coworker to administer it instead D) Discard it and draw a fresh, labeled dose
KEY: D. A trusts memory over labels (the exact failure the rule prevents); B launders an unverifiable syringe into a "labeled" one; C moves the same risk to different hands.

### MED-004 | QB-math | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: Order 75 mg; stock 50 mg/mL. Draw:
A) 1.5 mL B) 0.75 mL C) 0.67 mL D) 2.5 mL
KEY: A. B is the inverted-fraction slip; C divides the wrong direction (50/75); D misreads stock as 30 mg/mL-style mental math.

### MED-005 | QB-math | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: Order 12.5 mg; stock is 25 mg scored tablets. Dispense per dose:
A) One tablet B) Two tablets C) Half a tablet D) A quarter tablet
KEY: C. A and B are multiplication-direction slips; D is the "halve it twice to be safe" instinct — underdosing is still a dosing error.

### MED-006 | QB-math | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: A 2% solution contains how many mg/mL?
A) 2 B) 200 C) 0.2 D) 20
KEY: D — X% = X g/100 mL, so 2 g/100 mL = 20 mg/mL. A reads % as mg/mL directly; B slips a decimal the dangerous direction; C slips it the other way.

### MED-007 | QB-math | D3 | L4 | SC:Y
Q: Your calculator says 23 mL; your ballpark said "around 2." The syringes on the shelf top out at 12 mL. What is the strongest interpretation?
A) Draw two syringes of 11.5 mL each to reach the total B) A decimal or concentration error is likely — stop and re-verify C) Trust the calculation — estimates are rougher than calculators D) Round down to a single 12 mL syringe as a compromise
KEY: B. A engineers around a red flag; C inverts the purpose of estimation (the ballpark exists to catch the calculator's INPUT errors); D compromises between a number and a mistake.

### MED-008 | TS-MED-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: An insulin order reads "give 4." The U-100 syringes are missing; U-40 syringes are stocked. You:
A) Use the U-40 syringe as marked — 4 units reads as 4 units B) Convert the concentrations: draw to the 10-unit mark on the U-40 C) Stop — the syringe must match the vial's concentration; resolve with CVT/DVM D) Draw 0.04 mL in a TB syringe using the mL equivalent instead
KEY: C. A ignores that syringe graduations assume a concentration; B does real math but the improvised-conversion habit is exactly how insulin disasters happen; D is another confident improvisation outside your role.

### MED-009 | TS-MED-PO-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: Which patient can safely receive an oral tablet right now?
A) Laterally recumbent, heavily sedated post-op B) Sternal, alert, growling at the kennel front C) Actively seizing but between clusters D) Dyspneic with open-mouth breathing
KEY: B — grumpy is a restraint problem, not a swallowing problem. A, C, D are all aspiration setups; the item forces "unpleasant but safe" over "quiet but contraindicated."

### MED-010 | TS-MED-PO-001 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Dry-pilling a cat with doxycycline risks:
A) Esophagitis and stricture — follow with water or food B) Vomiting triggered by the tablet's bitter coating C) Reduced absorption from the dose sitting too high D) Tooth staining if the dosing repeats for weeks
KEY: A. B and C are plausible-sounding pharmacology; D borrows a true doxycycline fact from a different context (juvenile teeth) — the retained-capsule esophageal injury is the taught hazard.

### MED-011 | TS-INJ-SQ-001 | D2 | L2 | SC:Y
Q: You aspirate before an SQ injection because blood return would mean:
A) The needle nicked a capillary and needs a new angle B) The vaccine has drawn blood proteins in C) The patient's pressure is high today D) The tip is intravascular — withdraw and restart
KEY: D. A minimizes the finding into a re-angle; B and C are pseudo-physiology. Wrong-compartment delivery is the risk aspiration screens for.

### MED-012 | TS-INJ-IM-001 | D2 | L2 | SC:Y
Q: The IM site banned at CVAH is the:
A) Epaxial muscle group B) Quadriceps C) Caudal thigh D) Triceps region
KEY: C — sciatic nerve proximity; permanent lameness risk. A is the preferred default; B is an accepted alternative; D isn't a standard small-animal IM site but isn't the banned one.

### MED-013 | TS-INJ-SQ-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: You suspect half the vaccine went through the skin tent — the fur is wet. You:
A) Repeat the full dose; partial vaccination risks failure B) Repeat half the dose to make up the difference C) Note it in the chart and move on D) Report it — the re-dose decision belongs to the DVM
KEY: D. A and B both self-prescribe (with confident-sounding rationales); C hides a dosing uncertainty inside paperwork. "Report, don't decide" is the whole competency.

### MED-014 | TS-MED-PO-001 | D1 | L1 | SC:N
Q: "OS" on an ophthalmic order means:
A) Left eye B) Both eyes C) Right eye D) Left ear
KEY: A. C is OD; B is OU; D is AS — the parallel ear abbreviations are the classic confusion set.

### MED-015 | TS-MED-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: The strongest single habit against look-alike/sound-alike errors:
A) Memorizing which shelf each drug lives on B) Reading the full label and concentration aloud at each check C) Recognizing vials by cap color and size D) Having the same person always draw the meds
KEY: B. A and C are precisely the habits that CAUSE LASA errors (location and appearance identification); D concentrates rather than checks — familiarity is not verification.

### MED-016 | TS-INJ-SQ-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: Ten minutes after a vaccine, the puppy's muzzle is swelling and hives are rising. First move:
A) Antihistamine from the shelf — minutes matter B) Cold compress and a note for the doctor's next break C) Alert the DVM now — this is the anaphylaxis pathway D) Watch for 15 minutes to see if it progresses
KEY: C. A treats without an order (and under-treats anaphylaxis); B and D downgrade a time-critical pattern into routine care. Facial swelling post-vaccine is a now-event.

### MED-017 | TS-MED-001 | D2 | L2 | SC:Y
Q: For controlled substances, your role includes:
A) Witnessed draws and immediate log entries per procedure B) Independent draws if the key is available to you C) End-of-shift batch logging of the day's draws D) Verbal tracking between trusted coworkers
KEY: A. B confuses access with authorization; C and D are the drift patterns DEA-registrant procedures exist to prevent — the log is real-time and witnessed or it is wrong.

### MED-018 | QB-math | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: 8 mg/kg for a 22 lb dog (1 kg = 2.2 lb). Total dose:
A) 176 mg B) 80 mg C) 8 mg D) 36 mg
KEY: B — 22/2.2 = 10 kg × 8. A skips the unit conversion (the single most common real error); C forgets weight entirely; D divides by 2.2 twice.

### MED-019 | QB-math | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: 80 mg needed from 100 mg/mL stock. Draw:
A) 1.25 mL B) 8 mL C) 0.8 mL D) 0.08 mL
KEY: C. A inverts the fraction; B multiplies instead of divides; D adds a stray decimal — each is a recognizable arithmetic path, which is why the second-person math check exists.

### MED-020 | TS-MED-001 | D3 | L4 | SC:Y
Q: You realize you gave the wrong patient a probiotic capsule an hour ago. Both patients are fine. Reporting it now mainly accomplishes:
A) Protecting you professionally if the owner later finds out B) Getting the DVM to examine the patient for any reaction C) Feeding the near-miss system that prevents the dangerous version of this error D) Correcting both patients' medical records so billing stays accurate
KEY: C — blame-free error reporting exists to catch the SYSTEM gap (how did the wrong patient get your attention?) before it recurs with insulin. A, B, D are each real but minor; picking them shows the learner hasn't internalized why concealment is the terminable offense while the error itself is not.

### MED-021 | TS-MED-PO-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: Two different drops are due in the same eye at 8:00. You:
A) Space them 5 minutes apart, order per the DVM's direction B) Give both back-to-back and chart 8:00 C) Give one at 8:00 and one at noon D) Mix a drop of each on a sterile surface first
KEY: A. B washes the first drop out with the second; C rewrites the schedule; D compounds at the bedside. The 5-minute rule is small, specific, and constantly violated.

### MED-022 | TS-MED-001 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: The three label checks happen at:
A) Shelf, after drawing, and at patient-side with the verifier B) Shelf, at patient-side, and at charting C) After drawing, at patient-side, and at disposal D) Shelf, after drawing, and at end of shift
KEY: A. Each wrong option keeps three checks but moves one somewhere useless — after administration (B's charting, C's disposal, D's shift-end) is too late to stop anything.

### MED-023 | QB-math | D3 | L4 | SC:N
Q: 2.5 mg/kg BID for a 6 kg cat; stock is 15 mg scored tablets. Per DOSE the patient gets:
A) Half a tablet B) One tablet C) Two tablets D) One and a half tablets
KEY: B — 15 mg per dose = exactly one tablet. A is the per-day/per-dose confusion (feels safer, underdoses); C doubles for BID by mistake; D splits the difference between two errors.

### MED-024 | TS-INJ-IM-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: Mid-IM-injection aspiration returns a flash of blood. You've delivered nothing yet. Correct sequence:
A) Redirect the tip slightly and inject slowly B) Withdraw fully, replace the needle, choose a new site C) Inject anyway — IM drugs tolerate vessels D) Withdraw halfway and re-aspirate at the new depth
KEY: B. A and D stay in a vascular field hoping geometry saves them; C is false for several IM-intended drugs. Fresh needle, fresh site is the taught reflex.

### MED-025 | TS-MED-001 | D1 | L2 | SC:N
Q: "Right response" as a medication right means:
A) The patient accepted the medication willingly B) The chart entry was completed on time C) The verifier agreed with your math D) Observing and recording effect and adverse events afterward
KEY: D. A confuses compliance with response; B and C are the documentation and verification rights wearing this one's name.
