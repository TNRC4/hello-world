# QB-CORE-SUR — Surgery & Asepsis (15 items)
Rewritten to Assessment_Item_Quality_Standard 2026-08.

### SUR-001 | TS-SUR-STER-001 | D1 | L2 | SC:Y
Q: You THINK your sleeve brushed the drape edge, but you're not certain. The rule:
A) Certainty is required before disrupting a procedure B) Doubt equals contaminated — declare it C) Watch the spot and declare only if someone else noticed D) Re-glove quietly at the next natural pause
KEY: B. A and C set an evidence bar sterility doesn't use; D fixes half the problem silently — the field never learns.

### SUR-002 | TS-SUR-STER-001 | D1 | L1 | SC:N
Q: The non-sterile zone of an opened sterile wrapper:
A) The outer 2.5 cm border B) The underside only C) Any visibly wrinkled area D) The corner where the tape was
KEY: A. B, C, D substitute intuition for the defined border.

### SUR-003 | TS-SUR-PREP-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: The whiteboard says right stifle; the record says left. The patient is premedicated and time is tight. You:
A) Prep the right — the board is updated at morning rounds B) Prep the left — the record is the legal document C) Full stop; nothing is clipped until the DVM resolves it D) Clip both conservatively so either works
KEY: C. A and B pick a winner in a conflict that must be RESOLVED; D turns one wrong-site risk into a certainty of extra clipping and lingering ambiguity.

### SUR-004 | TS-SUR-PREP-001 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: The scrub pattern is:
A) Incision line outward in a spiral, new gauze each pass B) Edges inward, so dirt moves toward the drape line C) Straight overlapping rows like mowing D) Whole site at once with a soaked sponge
KEY: A. B reverses the contamination gradient; C and D describe cleaning, not aseptic prep.

### SUR-005 | TS-SUR-STER-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: External indicator tape turned; the internal strip did not. The pack is:
A) Usable — tape proves the cycle ran B) Usable for non-critical wounds only C) Suspect but acceptable if the load log looks right D) Not trusted — report and reprocess
KEY: D — tape shows outside exposure only. A, B, C are three depths of the same rationalization.

### SUR-006 | TS-SUR-PREP-001 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: Mid-clip, the blade feels hot against your wrist test. You:
A) Swap or cool it, check the skin, report any lesion B) Slow your stroke rate and continue C) Spray blade coolant and keep clipping immediately D) Finish the small remaining area first
KEY: A. B, C, D all keep hot metal on skin; C's coolant helps the NEXT pass, not the burn already brewing.

### SUR-007 | W5-04 | D1 | L1 | SC:N
Q: Heavy fascia is cut with:
A) Metzenbaum scissors B) Mayo scissors C) Iris scissors D) Suture scissors
KEY: B. A is the delicate-tissue instrument this question protects; C is ophthalmic-fine; D is for suture ends.

### SUR-008 | W5-04 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Autoclave chambers are loaded:
A) Tightly, to sterilize more per cycle B) With instruments unwrapped for direct steam C) Loosely, so steam circulates D) With packs stacked flat in contact
KEY: C. A and D are the efficiency instincts that sterilize the outside of a pile; B skips the wrap that keeps things sterile afterward.

### SUR-009 | W5-02 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: As circulator, you need to pass behind two scrubbed people standing back-to-back with a narrow gap. You:
A) Slip through sideways, facing one of their backs B) Announce and take the long way around C) Ask one to step forward so you can pass D) Pass quickly at a crouch below field level
KEY: B. A and D make contact odds a matter of luck; C moves a sterile person to solve YOUR routing problem — acceptable occasionally, but the long way is the default.

### SUR-010 | TS-SUR-STER-001 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Flip-dropping an item onto the field, your hands:
A) Stay outside the field's vertical plane, dropping from 15–30 cm at the edge B) Reach just past the edge so the drop lands accurately C) Hover centrally so the item lands mid-field, clear of edges D) Lower the item slowly to the field to minimize air disturbance
KEY: A. B and C cross the plane; D means your hands travel WITH the item — the slow version of the same violation.

### SUR-011 | W5-02 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: The recovery bay is set up:
A) During closing, when timing is predictable B) Before induction C) At extubation D) While the patient is moved from the table
KEY: B — recovery is the risk phase; the others all discover missing warming and suction with the patient already waking.

### SUR-012 | TS-SUR-PREP-001 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Prep solution pooling under a small patient causes:
A) Faster site drying B) Better antiseptic contact time C) Chemical irritation and hypothermia D) Grounding-pad interference only
KEY: C. A and B convert the hazard into a benefit; D names one real sub-issue and misses the patient-level two.

### SUR-013 | W5-04 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Instruments after surgery are:
A) Soaked overnight in saline to loosen debris B) Rinsed soon, enzymatic per label, brushed hinges-open, inspected C) Wiped dry and autoclaved directly D) Run through the ultrasonic with blood in place
KEY: B. A pits stainless steel (saline is corrosive); C bakes debris on; D asks the ultrasonic to do the pre-clean's job.

### SUR-014 | W5-05 | D2 | L2 | SC:Y
Q: The anesthesia phase with the quiet mortality reputation:
A) Recovery B) Induction C) Maintenance D) Premedication
KEY: A — vigilance collapses exactly when brachycephalics obstruct; B is the dramatic phase people already watch.

### SUR-015 | W5-05 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: You notice the reservoir bag hasn't moved for a while; the anesthetist is turned to the monitor stack. You:
A) Wait for them to finish — they're mid-task B) Say "bag isn't moving" now, loud enough to act on C) Reach over and give the bag a test squeeze D) Check the SpO2 first so your report is complete
KEY: B. A defers to focus during a possible apnea; C touches equipment that isn't yours to squeeze uninstructed; D gathers data while seconds pass — the observation IS the report.
