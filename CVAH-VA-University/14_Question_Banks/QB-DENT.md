# QB-DENT — Dentistry Support Academy (12 items)
Rewritten to Assessment_Item_Quality_Standard 2026-08.

### DENT-001 | DEN-M1 | D1 | L1 | SC:N
Q: In the Triadan system, the 400s are the:
A) Right upper quadrant B) Left lower quadrant C) Right lower quadrant D) Left upper quadrant
KEY: C — 100s right upper, 200s left upper, 300s left lower, 400s right lower; the options are the four ways to misremember the circle.

### DENT-002 | DEN-M1 | D2 | L1 | SC:N
Q: The upper carnassial in the adult dog is tooth:
A) 108 B) 104 C) 208 and only 208 D) 109
KEY: A — upper fourth premolar (208 is its left twin, so C's "only" fails); B is the canine; D is the first molar.

### DENT-003 | DEN-M2 | D2 | L2 | SC:Y
Q: Spring-loaded mouth gags in cats are:
A) Standard equipment for surgical visibility during extractions B) Restricted per safety consensus — maxillary artery compression and blindness risk C) Acceptable when the spring tension is documented below threshold D) Preferred over foam props for positional stability in long procedures
KEY: B. A and D describe the old normal; C invents a threshold that doesn't rescue the mechanism.

### DENT-004 | DEN-M3 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: The bisecting-angle technique exists because:
A) Sensors are too fragile to wedge into parallel placement B) It halves the number of exposures a full-mouth series needs C) It compensates for the slight motion of an anesthetized patient D) Oral anatomy blocks parallel placement for many views; the beam bisects the tooth-sensor angle
KEY: D. A, B, C attach the technique to equipment, efficiency, and motion — none of which is the geometry problem.

### DENT-005 | DEN-M3 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: Your dental rad shows roots stretched far longer than reality. The fix:
A) Increase the exposure and retake B) Re-angle — the beam was too parallel to the tooth axis C) Re-angle — the beam was too perpendicular to the sensor D) Move the sensor deeper into the mouth
KEY: B — elongation = undershot bisect; C describes foreshortening's fix (the paired error); A and D change the wrong variables.

### DENT-006 | DEN-M2 | D2 | L2 | SC:Y
Q: Dental patients trend hypothermic because:
A) Dental suites run colder than ORs B) The drugs used for dentals suppress thermoregulation more C) Long anesthesia plus continuous water plus evaporative loss D) Open-mouth breathing bypasses nasal warming
KEY: C — the combination is the mechanism; A, B, D each offer one lonely, mostly-wrong factor.

### DENT-007 | DEN-M4 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Polishing after scaling exists to:
A) Smooth micro-etched enamel that would re-accrete plaque faster B) Remove the residual stain that scaling alone leaves behind C) Drive fluoride from the paste deeper into the enamel surface D) Mark the procedure complete for the chart's cleaning entry
KEY: A. B and C are side effects at best; D is cargo-cult workflow.

### DENT-008 | DEN-M2 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: You notice water pooling in the pharynx during a dental. You:
A) Note it in the record — the cuffed tube makes pooling harmless B) Tip the table slightly so gravity drains it away from the airway C) Alert the operator/anesthetist — suction and packs are theirs, vigilance is yours D) Suction it yourself quickly in the pause between their instruments
KEY: C. A over-trusts cuffs; B repositions an anesthetized airway on your own authority; D puts uninvited hands in a working field.

### DENT-009 | DEN-M4 | D2 | L1 | SC:N
Q: Dull hand scalers are dangerous mainly because:
A) Their rough edges scratch and score the enamel surface B) They force harder strokes — slips and tissue trauma C) Their worn surfaces harbor bacteria that sharpening removes D) They only lengthen the anesthesia time by a few minutes
KEY: B. A and C are imagined mechanisms; D understates the cascade dullness causes.

### DENT-010 | DEN-M1 | D2 | L1 | SC:N
Q: Periodontal disease progresses as:
A) Gingivitis → plaque → calculus → attachment loss B) Calculus → plaque → gingivitis → attachment loss C) Plaque → gingivitis → attachment loss → calculus D) Plaque → calculus → gingivitis → attachment loss
KEY: D — the sequence items scramble; knowing it makes the procedure make sense.

### DENT-011 | DEN-M3 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: Dental sensors and plates are:
A) Autoclaved between patients like instruments B) Sleeved per patient, never autoclaved, cables protected C) Wiped with alcohol and air-dried D) Soaked in cold sterile between cases
KEY: B. A, C, D destroy or under-protect a four-figure item in three different ways.

### DENT-012 | DEN-M2 | D3 | L4 | SC:Y
Q: 90 minutes into a dental: temp 97.1°F drifting down, HR slowing in parallel. Your read and move:
A) Report the paired trend now — hypothermia deepens anesthesia; escalate warming per direction B) Normal long-procedure drift — chart both values and continue C) Flag the heart rate; the temperature is background context D) Speed your instrument handling so the procedure finishes sooner
KEY: A — temperature is an anesthetic drug in disguise, and the paired slope is the story. B and C each dismiss half the pattern; D helps in minutes when the problem is now.
