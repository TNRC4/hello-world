# QB-ECC — Emergency & Critical Care Academy (15 items; extends QB-CORE-EMR)
Rewritten to Assessment_Item_Quality_Standard 2026-08.

### ECC-001 | ECC-M1 | D2 | L2 | SC:Y
Q: Primary-survey observations are reported in which order?
A) Circulation, breathing, airway — worst first B) Airway, breathing, circulation C) Whatever is most abnormal first D) Temperature, pulse, respiration
KEY: B. C sounds clinically mature but breaks the shared team sequence; A inverts it; D is the wellness-exam frame, not the survey.

### ECC-002 | ECC-M2 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: Oxygen for a fractious, dyspneic cat is best delivered by:
A) A snug mask held firmly for a good seal B) Brief intubation by the assistant C) Flow-by or oxygen cage per direction D) Waiting — handling risks outweigh oxygen benefits
KEY: C — stress kills dyspneic cats; A adds a fight to the hypoxia; B is out of scope; D abandons the one safe intervention.

### ECC-003 | TS-ECC-102 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: For a barrel-chested bulldog, RECOVER conformation guidance points toward:
A) Standard lateral technique — conformation doesn't change BLS B) Dorsal-recumbency compression consideration per the team lead C) Circumferential two-hand technique D) Compressions directly over the highest rib point in lateral
KEY: B. A denies the 2024 emphasis; C is the cat/small-dog method; D is the round-chest (not barrel) rule — the near-miss.

### ECC-004 | ECC-M3 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: Mid-CPR, EtCO2 jumps sharply upward. You:
A) Announce possible ROSC to the lead now B) Stop compressions to check a pulse C) Ask the ventilator to slow the breaths D) Note it for the debrief log quietly
KEY: A — announce; the LEAD schedules rhythm/pulse checks at cycle breaks. B interrupts perfusion on your own authority; C freelances the ventilation; D archives a live signal.

### ECC-005 | ECC-M2 | D2 | L2 | SC:Y
Q: "Stage the emergency IV setup" means:
A) Place the catheter yourself so access is ready when the DVM arrives B) Clippers, prep, two catheter sizes, primed T-port, flush, torn tape, spiked fluids — laid out for the placer C) Bring the IV pole, a spiked bag of fluids, and a primed line to the table D) Clip and prep the limb fully, leaving the placement setup to the CVT
KEY: B — staging is complete and hands-off. A crosses into placement on a crashing patient; C and D stage fragments.

### ECC-006 | ECC-M1 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: Simultaneous arrivals: (1) walking dog with a bleeding paw laceration, (2) open-mouth-breathing cat, (3) bright wiggly puppy that vomited once. Triage order:
A) 1, 2, 3 — control the visible bleeding first B) 2, 1, 3 C) 3, 2, 1 — youngest first D) In arrival order, to be fair
KEY: B — airway/breathing outranks visible-but-minor hemorrhage; A is the blood-catches-the-eye error; C and D aren't triage.

### ECC-007 | ECC-M4 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: Heat-stroke cooling by assistants is done:
A) With an ice-water immersion for fastest core drop B) With tepid water and airflow per DVM direction, stopping at the ordered target C) Only after the DVM has examined the patient fully D) With alcohol applied to the paws as the primary method
KEY: B. A vasoconstricts and shivers heat back in; C delays a time-critical intervention the DVM will order in seconds; D promotes an adjunct to a strategy.

### ECC-008 | ECC-M3 | D2 | L1 | SC:Y
Q: Intubated CPR ventilation runs at about:
A) 10 breaths/min, avoiding hyperventilation B) 20 breaths/min for better oxygenation C) 2 breaths per 30 compressions D) One breath per compression pause
KEY: A. B feels helpful and costs venous return; C is the NON-intubated ratio; D describes a pattern RECOVER removed.

### ECC-009 | ECC-M4 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: A stable rodenticide-exposure dog needs bloodwork. Your anticipation includes:
A) A citrate tube in the draw setup and extended pressure at puncture sites B) A jugular draw for the cleanest coag sample C) Warming the limb well to speed the draw D) An arterial sample kit in case gases are needed
KEY: A — coag testing likely, coagulopathy-aware technique mandatory. B chooses the one site coagulopathy forbids; C and D are beside the point.

### ECC-010 | ECC-M2 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: The crash cart's dosing chart is:
A) A memorization target for ECC credential holders B) The DVM's private reference C) A versioned, DVM-approved controlled card you locate and present D) A guideline the team adapts case by case
KEY: C — never quoted from memory, never adapted on the fly (D), and everyone should know where it lives (B), while memorizing doses (A) invites unauthorized use.

### ECC-011 | ECC-M5 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: The recorder's log captures:
A) A clean narrative summary written immediately after the code ends B) Timestamped events verbatim as called — cycles, drugs as stated, rhythm calls C) The interventions that worked, so the record shows the effective path D) The lead's dictated recap captured at each two-minute cycle break
KEY: B. A trades accuracy for neatness; C curates history; D makes the lead do the recorder's job.

### ECC-012 | ECC-M1 | D2 | L3 | SC:Y
Q: A hit-by-car arrives breathing hard; the owner insists on completing paperwork first. You:
A) Help them finish quickly — consent matters B) Take the patient back now; a teammate handles the owner with compassion C) Have the owner carry the patient back so both move D) Get a verbal consent recording before moving
KEY: B — triage policy moves the patient; the relationship is handled in parallel, not in sequence (A, D), and owners don't belong in treatment during stabilization (C).

### ECC-013 | ECC-M3 | D2 | L2 | SC:N
Q: The best live feedback on compression quality comes from:
A) The compressor's sense of effort B) Elapsed time within the cycle C) The depth the sternum visibly moves D) EtCO2 trend plus the lead's assessment
KEY: D. A fails exactly when fatigue does; B and C are single inputs the trend integrates.

### ECC-014 | ECC-M4 | D2 | L3 | SC:N
Q: Staging for an inbound blocked cat includes ECG readiness because:
A) Blocked cats often need sedation, which requires monitoring B) Hyperkalemia can make these hearts arrhythmic and fragile C) The DVM will want a baseline rhythm for the record D) Urinary catheters can trigger vagal responses later
KEY: B — the potassium-heart link is the reason this staging habit exists; A, C, D are true-ish uses that miss the danger.

### ECC-015 | ECC-M5 | D3 | L3 | SC:N
Q: A post-code debrief exists primarily to:
A) Identify who made which errors while memories are still fresh B) Complete the medical record accurately from the recorder's log C) Reconstruct the timeline, find system jams, verify restock, check on the team D) Decide as a group whether the outcome was ultimately preventable
KEY: C — system learning plus people care. A and D turn it into a tribunal; B is one output, not the purpose.
