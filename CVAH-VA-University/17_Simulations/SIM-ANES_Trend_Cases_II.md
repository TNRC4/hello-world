# SIM-ANES-007..014 — Anesthesia Simulation Library, Series II
Progressive-disclosure format: values reveal in stages; the learner commits answers at each stage BEFORE the next reveal (LMS: branching/lesson tool; tabletop: card flips). Five questions at every stage: What changed? What changed FIRST? Real, artifact-possible, or equipment? What do I physically check? What do I say, to whom, how urgently? ≥85% across the series for T3. Values are teaching constructs; live thresholds per CRC-003.

## SIM-ANES-007 "The Boring Neuter" (the normal case — deliberately)
Healthy 1y lab neuter. Stages walk a NORMAL case start to finish: stable values wandering within bands (HR 88→96→90, MAP 68→74→70, EtCO2 38→42, temp drifting 101.2→99.8 over 40 min). Teaching goals: (a) trend-reading includes recognizing normal wander vs directional walk; (b) the ONE reportable item hides in plain sight — temp slope earning a warming mention by stage 3; (c) over-reporting calibration: learners who escalate the HR wobble get the explanation of WHY it isn't a report. Not every scenario ends in crisis; most shifts don't.

## SIM-ANES-008 "Feline Dental, Hour Two"
3.8 kg senior cat, dental. Stage 1: T 98.9 (was 100.6), HR 150 (was 175), all else stable. Stage 2 (+15 min): T 97.8, HR 132, MAP softening 70→62, depth "seems deeper" per anesthetist at unchanged vaporizer. Stage 3: T 96.9, HR 118, slow capnograph amplitude decline. Spine: hypothermia as the engine dragging everything — first-change = temperature at stage 1; correct arc = warming escalation EARLY + trend report, not three separate "number reports." Classic error scored: treating the HR fall as the primary finding.

## SIM-ANES-009 "The Pediatric Cliff"
1.1 kg 10-week puppy, hernia repair. Stage 1: rock-stable but glucose-and-heat flags in the brief. Stage 2: 25 min in — subtle: RR 24→18, EtCO2 42→48, temp 99.1→97.9. Stage 3: pale-ish gums called by hands-on beat, HR climbing. Teaching spine: small patients move FAST and lag-free; the report belongs at stage 2 (two slopes agreeing); stage 3 reporters are late and told so. Also drills equipment-dead-space thinking from ANE-M1.

## SIM-ANES-010 "The Obese Spay's Pressure Problem"
42 kg obese lab spay. Oscillometric reads: 190/…, then 45/…, then error, then 88/… — chaotically. Pleth clean, pulses strong, gums pink throughout. Spine: this is the CUFF/conformation case — trust algorithm from ANE-L4: patient-check says fine, technique-check finds cuff too narrow for the limb cone; correct arc = verify → recuff/resite → report the methods problem and the believable baseline, NOT the garbage numbers. "What doesn't fit" = the machine, this time.

## SIM-ANES-011 "Bulldog Recovery" (the series' flagship — mirrors CSC-ANE-01 §9)
Extubated bulldog, minute 0 fine. Stage 1 (+10): louder breathing "normal for the breed?" — learner must commit. Stage 2 (+18): RR up, visible effort, sitting up neck-extended. Stage 3 (+22): dusky gums. Spine: stage-1 commitment is the exam — "compare to THIS patient's baseline, position/watch continues, verbal flag now" scores full; "he's a bulldog, they all sound like that" is the scored trap. Stage 3 = emergency activation + O2 + re-intubation kit ready (anesthetist acts; you stage and call).

## SIM-ANES-012 "Emergency Splenectomy, Running Behind"
HBC dog, emergency sx, unstable from the start: bands set case-specific (MAP alert at 65, told at briefing). Values fluctuate around bands with surgeon events stamped (clamp on/off, bleeding moment). Spine: (a) case-specific bands OVERRIDE defaults — learners applying CRC-003 defaults under-report; (b) event-correlation: the MAP dip 90 s after "clamp off" is reported WITH its context; (c) the recorder-under-pressure skill: 4 drugs called in 6 minutes; record audited against script.

## SIM-ANES-013 "The Disconnection Nobody Felt"
Routine dental, mid-case table turn for rads. Stage 1: EtCO2 flat mid-rotation, SpO2 98, HR unchanged. Spine: the CSC-ANE-01 flatline drill under realistic cover ("we just moved the table — probably the line, right?"): correct arc = hands-and-circuit check WHILE announcing, found Y-piece disconnection, reconnect (that's a connection, within role), report completed-loop. Scored trap: silent line-jiggling; second trap: full arrest announcement without the 5-second circuit look — calibration cuts both ways.

## SIM-ANES-014 "Arrest" (capstone, runs as team tabletop + follows SIM-CORE-004 live mock)
Geriatric mass-removal. Stage 1: 20-min slow multi-parameter erosion (each value individually explainable — the insidious pattern). Stage 2: EtCO2 collapses to 8 with intact waveform, pulses lost. Stage 3: RECOVER activation — roles, cycles, EtCO2-as-compression-feedback, ROSC recognition (EtCO2 jump). Spine: stage 1 is where the exam lives — learners who assembled the erosion into a report before the cliff pass at distinction; stage 2–3 measure role fluency under the code. Debrief per ECC doctrine.

## "WHAT DOESN'T FIT?" standing exercise set (WDF-01..08; 5-min drills, rotated into every Academy week)
WDF-01 ECG 96 organized / femoral thin and 60-ish → electricity vs squeeze; verify pulse, report mismatch NOW. · WDF-02 SpO2 99 / RR 5 and shallow / EtCO2 61 → oxygenation ≠ ventilation; report hypoventilation trend. · WDF-03 Oscillometric 40 / patient pink, strong pulses → trust algorithm; technique first. · WDF-04 Capnogram shrinking / vaporizer untouched → depth? circuit? sampling? — the check ladder. · WDF-05 Monitor shows apnea / bag moving steadily → sampling line vs sensor; explain before dismissing. · WDF-06 Patient swallows during "adequate depth" / all numbers pretty → hands beat screens; report immediately. · WDF-07 Temp probe reads 103.8 on a cooling dental patient → probe displaced (where is it actually?); verify with rectal before reporting a fever. · WDF-08 Pleth beautiful / gums going pale → believe the gums, seek the third witness (pulse, BP), report while checking. Format: 60 seconds, verbal answer, scored on verify-first sequencing and urgency. These eight rotate with new ones written from real case events (near-miss reports feed the library).
